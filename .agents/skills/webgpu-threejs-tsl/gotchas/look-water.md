# Look: water, mirrors and reflections

Read before building or changing water, a lake, a sea, a wet floor or anything that mirrors its surroundings.

**The recurring lesson.** A mirror shows slope, not height: every facet, seam and texel edge in the normal is visible, and an offset that is too large tears the image. And a planar `reflector()` re-renders the scene, so the sky never goes through it.

**Which mirror for what.**

| What the water must show | Use | Row |
|---|---|---|
| A sky that is a cheap function of a direction (gradient, sun, stars) | Call the same sky function again along the mirrored ray: exact, full resolution, no pass | #137 |
| A sky that is expensive (a marched cloud or aurora) | Paint it ONCE into a reduced-resolution target; the backdrop shows the target and the water looks its mirrored ray up in it. Needs the horizon below mid-frame: check portrait | #128 |
| Things standing in the scene (trees, a shore, creatures) | A `reflector()` restricted to their camera layer, cleared to alpha 0, laid over the mirrored sky; `bounces: false`, the scene pass's `samples` | #137, #96, #224 |
| A few simple near things (a mast, a bolt) | Draw them a second time, flipped, in the vertex stage: no pass at all | #128 |

One gotcha per line; the number is the row's permanent id (cited elsewhere as "gotcha #NN"). Rows numbered below 300 were numbered before these files existed and keep their numbers, in no order. A NEW row goes at the END of this table and takes the next free number in **1300–1399**. To search: `node .claude/skills/webgpu-threejs-tsl/scripts/gotcha.mjs <words>` (`README.md` in this folder).

| #203 | Need sun glitter on water (points of light that come and go in the sun's path) that stays in the column, keeps its size at any range and does not shimmer as the camera drifts | — | Light hashed cells by the CHANCE that a ripple facet there is tilted to the halfway vector: with slope spread σ, `odds = exp(-tan²θ_h / 2σ²)` where `tan²θ_h = (h.x² + h.z²) / h.y²`, and a cell lights when its hash exceeds `1 - odds`. That alone draws the sun's column narrow across and long toward the eye, and a breeze (a larger σ) widens it; a `pow(dot(reflect(...), sun), n)` envelope instead either smears or scatters the points over the whole lake. Lay the cells out in the angles the eye sees, measured from the RESTING eye (`(x / -z, eyeHeight / horizontalDistance) * cellsPerTangent`, set when the camera is framed), give each a Gaussian spot and a twinkle `pow(saturate(sin(t·rate + phase)), 6)`, and gate it by the shadow. Found on Golden Forest's second pass (2026-10-20), the glitter in `src/themes/golden-forest/golden-forest-lake.js` |
| #128 | Need a flat water plane to mirror an expensive sky (a marched cloud, an aurora) without rendering the sky twice | — | Paint the sky once into its own (reduced-resolution) `RenderTarget` with a `QuadMesh` whose fragment builds the view ray from camera-basis uniforms, show it with a full-screen backdrop that samples the target at `screenUV`, and let the water look its MIRRORED ray up in the same target: `clip = cameraProjectionMatrix · cameraViewMatrix · vec4(reflect(view, normal), 0)` (a direction, so `w = 0`), `uv = (clip.x / clip.w · 0.5 + 0.5, 0.5 − clip.y / clip.w · 0.5)`, clamped. It is exact for anything far away and holds whenever the horizon sits below the middle of the frame (the mirrored ray then stays inside it). Near things (masts, bolts) are drawn a second time upside down in the vertex stage instead. Both backends. Precedent: `voltage-storm-sky.js`, `voltage-storm-water.js` |
| #137 | A planar `reflector()` doubles the cost of an expensive sky, and its reduced-resolution image blurs the horizon, the stars and anything else far away in the water | The mirror pass re-renders the whole scene, the sky dome's shader included, into a small target | Write the whole distance as ONE TSL function of a direction and call it twice: the dome along the view ray, the water along its mirror ray (exact, full resolution, bends with the surface's slope, no pass). Mirror only what stands IN the scene: put it on a camera layer, restrict the mirror to it (`reflection.reflector.getVirtualCamera(camera).layers.set(L)` after `camera.layers.enable(L)`), clear to nothing (`renderer.setClearColor(0x000000, 0)`) and lay it over the mirrored sky in the water, `sky * (1 - held.a) + held.rgb`. Additive parts must leave alpha alone (`outputNode = vec4(rgb, 0)`), or they black out the mirrored sky behind their quads. Precedent: `vcBackdrop` in `src/themes/vesper-chrysalis/vesper-chrysalis-tsl.js`, `vesper-chrysalis-lake.js` (2026-10-09) |
| #138 | A water mirror shows flat facets or blocks near the camera | Two causes that look alike. The slope is read from a noise texture magnified so far that one bilinear texel spans tens of pixels: the value is continuous but its gradient steps at every texel edge, and a mirror shows a gradient. Or a reduced-resolution `reflector()` is sampled at a higher mip (`.level(n)`) for softness, which magnifies a tiny image | Keep the slope noise's texel under about ten centimetres where the camera stands and carry the longest wave as a sum of sines; sample the reflector at level 0 (`generateMipmaps: false` also saves the mip chain every frame) and let the slope do the softening. `src/themes/vesper-chrysalis/vesper-chrysalis-tsl.js`, `vesper-chrysalis-lake.js` |
| #141 | The picture in a `reflector()` mirror tears into marbled swirls when several ripples overlap, worst near the camera | The mirror's screen-space offset was the summed slope of every ring times a constant: three overlapping rings of slope 0.5 shift the lookup by a third of the screen | Keep ripple slopes physical (0.1 to 0.2), clamp the offset itself before it is added to `screenUV` (`clamp(slope, -0.09, 0.09)`), and let a crest's own light make a ring read, not a larger slope. Same rebuild, `src/themes/stillwater/stillwater-water.js` |
| #124 | Hard-edged dark or bright patches on a surface that mirrors a closed-form environment (a ray traced against an ideal pipe, sphere or box in the fragment shader); they follow the mesh's swell, not the light | The trace assumes its ray starts ON the ideal shape, but the mesh is displaced (a swell, a bend, a blend into another shape), so some of its points lie outside the shape; there the trace takes its "outside" branch (or misses) and answers for a different place | Draw the ray's start in toward the ideal shape's middle before tracing (scale its offset from the axis to just under 1), and leave the shading point where it is. Found on Waves (2026-10-09), the `pull` before `tubeTrace` in `src/themes/waves/waves-water.js` |
| #125 | A thin dark line runs down a mirror-like surface exactly where two halves of one generated sheet meet (a floor and the wall that rises from it), though the positions agree there | The halves agree in position but not in SLOPE: one carried a displacement term the other faded out differently, so the normal turns by a degree or two across the seam, and a mirror shows a degree | Make both halves reach the seam with the same height AND zero slope: fade every displacement to nothing over a short run before the seam on both sides (a Gaussian in the distance from it, not a linear ramp), and take tangents on each side from that side's own neighbours. Same rebuild, `createWaveShape` in `src/themes/waves/waves-tsl.js` |
| #210 | A surface with flowing water on it (a wave's face, a waterfall, a river) reads as a long-exposure photograph or brushed metal: every ripple a hairline, the sky it mirrors smeared into scratches | Its slope was read from a noise drawn out far along the flow (8:1, foam threads 80–125:1) and tilted only ACROSS the flow, so the normal varies in one direction only | Draw the noise out no more than about 3–4:1, give every scale a tilt along the flow as well as across it, add a fine chop near the eye (fade it with distance), and keep foam threads thin and rare. Precedent: the ripples in `src/themes/waves/waves-water.js` (2026-10-21) |
| #185 | Wind ripples on water seen from above form a regular lattice of fish scales | Several coherent sine trains with straight phases interfere into crescent cells | Domain-warp each train's phase with low-frequency noise (a few radians over hundreds of metres) and give the trains incommensurate wavelengths and headings; noise layers on top. High Coast (2026-10-13), `high-coast-sea.js` |
| #186 | Islands mirrored in the sea by a heightfield march have stair-stepped edges, and the mirrored land reads pale grey | A geometric march decides hit or miss per pixel at discrete steps, so the image's edge jumps from row to row; the mirrored colour got the eye-to-land aerial AND the water's own aerial (hazed twice) | Bisect between the last step above the land and the first below it (4 steps), keep the ray's smallest angular clearance over the land and fade the image in by it (`1 − smoothstep(0, 0.01, clearance / t)`), and give the mirrored land only the air between the water and the land (`mix(reflectedSky, land, exp(−σ t))`). Write the land test once: one `Loop` of `steps + 4` iterations that marches, then bisects (inlined in two loops it cost 7 KB of GLSL; a laid-out `Fn` would close over the field textures, which r186 caches module-wide). High Coast (2026-10-13), the mirror march in `high-coast-sea.js` |
| #208 | Need the underside of a water surface seen from below at a grazing angle (the top of an underwater frame) to read as rippling water, not a ceiling with cloud blobs | The window/mirror switch was driven by broad noise slopes: at the edge of Snell's window a slope of a few hundredths flips a facet between sky and mirror, so a 5 m noise feature is a 200 px blob | Make the surface the HEIGHT of the caustic pattern the light pass already paints (three reads a few centimetres apart at one explicit level, faded out where `fwidth(xz)` says the threads are under a pixel): each thread becomes a facet pair, one into the window and one out, so the surface crinkles at the net's scale and its bright facets stand over the shafts and the net below. Settle wave trains by pixels per wavelength (`fwidth`), let the lost tilt widen the window's `smoothstep` instead, open the window per channel at three indices for a fringe, and drop the broadest chop. Precedent: `createSurface` in `src/themes/ocean/ocean-surface.js` (2026-10-21) |
| #156 | Light shafts drawn in post and caustics drawn on the ground do not line up: a shaft lands beside its bright patch, or the two move to different water | Each was its own pattern (a radial blur from the sun, a noise on the ground) | Paint ONE tileable light pattern per frame into a small `RenderTarget` (caustic net in one channel, the shafts' broad mask in another, `wrapS/T = Repeat`, mipmaps) and let everything look it up at the point where the sun's ray through it entered the surface: `xz + sunDir.xz * (SURFACE - y) / sunDir.y`. The ground reads it per pixel, the post stack's march reads it at every step along the view ray (reduced-resolution `rtt`), so a shaft stands over its own patch. Read it three times a hand's breadth apart along the light for a coloured fringe. Precedent: `createLightPass`, `ocLightPoint` in `src/themes/ocean/ocean-tsl.js`, `buildShafts` in `ocean-post.js` (2026-10-11) |
| #259 | A lens effect drawn inside a sky function (a starburst, a flare spike) stands in the water too, bent by the ripples into a zigzag bolt | The water reads the same sky function along its mirrored ray, and the spikes were added with the scene's own light | Hand lens-only light back separately and add it only where the pixel looks at the sky directly (`spikes * (1 - wet)`); the mirror keeps the source's own glow. `spikes` in `src/themes/solar-eclipse/solar-eclipse-sun.js` and `-sky.js` (2026-10-21) |
| #238 | The sea line (or any far ground) reads as a ruled line under a glowing sky, though the far sea is hazed to "the horizon's colour" | The haze colour was mixed by hand (a share of the horizon colour, a share of the mid sky) and is darker than the sky just above the sea line, which also carries a band of light on the line itself; a fully hazed far sea and the sky meet with a step | Haze toward the sky's OWN colour at the sea line on that bearing: call the sky function along `normalize(vec3(view.x, 0, view.z))` (cheap variant, no stars) and `mix(col, thatSky, haze)`. The far sea then dissolves into the sky where they meet; thin the haze a little, since that colour is brighter. Precedent: `air` in `src/themes/fluid-dreams/fluid-dreams-liquid.js` (2026-10-20) |
