# Materials: alpha, blending, masks, depth and draw order

Read when choosing how a part blends and sorts, and when something draws behind what it should be in front of, leaves a box, comes out twice as bright, ignores its mask or vanishes with no error.

**House defaults.** Solids draw first and write depth; the sky or backdrop draws LAST among the opaque parts (`depthTest: true`, `depthWrite: false`); light draws after as premultiplied additive that leaves the target's alpha alone (`premultipliedAlpha: true`, `outputNode = vec4(rgb, 0)`). `renderOrder` goes on meshes, never on a `Group`.

One gotcha per line; the number is the row's permanent id (cited elsewhere as "gotcha #NN"). Rows numbered below 300 were numbered before these files existed and keep their numbers, in no order. A NEW row goes at the END of this table and takes the next free number in **600–699**. To search: `node .claude/skills/webgpu-threejs-tsl/scripts/gotcha.mjs <words>` (`README.md` in this folder).

| # | Symptom | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|---|
| #90 | An additive layer leaves a grey box, or changes a post effect that reads the scene pass's alpha | Non-premultiplied `AdditiveBlending` is `(SrcAlpha, One, One, One)` (`WebGPUPipelineUtils.js`): every additive fragment ADDS its alpha of 1 to the target | `premultipliedAlpha: true` (blend `One, One, One, One`) plus `material.outputNode = vec4(rgb, 0)`. `outputNode` replaces the result after the premultiply step, so the light is added and the target's alpha is left alone. Precedent: `black-hole-matter.js` |
| #91 | Alpha written by an opaque material's `colorNode` arrives as 1 | `NodeMaterial.setupDiffuseColor` assigns `diffuseColor.a = 1` for opaque, normally-blended materials | Write the whole fragment: `material.fragmentNode = vec4(rgb, a)`. Precedent: `black-hole-lens.js` hands the post chain a mask this way |
| #111 | A material with a `fragmentNode` ignores its `maskNode`: the cut-out shape draws as the whole quad or strip, with no error | `NodeMaterial.setup()` applies `maskNode` (a `discard`) inside `setupDiffuseColor()`, and that only runs while `fragmentNode` is null; a set `fragmentNode` replaces the whole path | Put the colour in `colorNode` (unlit `MeshBasicNodeMaterial`) and keep `maskNode`, or discard inside the fragment function yourself (`cond.discard()`) |
| #119 | A self-lit additive material (motes, sparks) is twice as bright as its `colorNode` says, and a bloom strength tuned with selective bloom is wrong without it | `MeshBasicNodeMaterial`'s output is diffuse **plus** emissive. Setting `colorNode = light` and `emissiveNode = light` (the usual way to feed selective bloom) adds the light to the frame twice, while the MRT `emissive` target gets it once | For a pure emitter make the diffuse term black: `material.colorNode = vec4(0, 0, 0, 1)` and put the light in `emissiveNode` only. The generated fragment shows it (`Output = DiffuseColor.rgb + EmissiveColor`). Found on Murmuration's motes (2026-10-08), `src/themes/murmuration/rendering/fluid-particles-renderer.js` |
| #206 | A layer set to draw early (`renderOrder` below its backdrop) still disappears behind the backdrop; no error | `renderOrder` on a `THREE.Group` is the GROUP order: three sorts every child of that group as one block before the scene's ungrouped objects (groupOrder first, then renderOrder), so a group at -99 drew before a sky dome at -100 and the dome painted over it | Put `renderOrder` on the meshes, not the group. Found on Odyssey's journey constellation (2026-11-01), `chapter-environments/urban-dreams.js` |
| #139 | The transparent draws of a part made of several meshes come out AFTER everything else, whatever their `renderOrder` (a lily pad paints over a moth in front of it); nothing errors | The part's meshes sit in a `THREE.Group` and the part's order was set on the Group. three sorts by a Group's renderOrder (`groupOrder`) BEFORE any mesh's own, so every child of a Group with order 10 sorts after every mesh under a Group with order 0 | Leave a Group's renderOrder at 0 and put the order on each mesh. Found by a unit test that compared the sorted draw list with the numbers, not by eye: `vcPart` in `src/themes/vesper-chrysalis/vesper-chrysalis-tsl.js` |
| #129 | Opaque-looking things in front of the sky turn pastel (kites, flags, banners, birds drawn as quads); lowering their brightness changes nothing | The material is `transparent` with `depthWrite: false`, and a post pass adds light by scene depth (a haze or light-shaft march, depth fog). Behind such a fragment the depth buffer still holds the sky, so it is given the in-scatter of the whole line of sight on top of its own colour | Write depth for anything that is really opaque: `depthWrite: true` plus a small `alphaTest` so a fragment that is faded out leaves no depth behind. Keep `depthWrite: false` for what is light itself (ribbons, motes, glows). Found on Verdant Hills' kites (2026-10-09), `src/themes/verdant-hills/verdant-hills-kites.js` |
| #265 | Layers of a canopy, a cloud deck or any shelf painted in a backdrop (a sky function) let geometry that rises through them (trunks, towers, masts) pass in FRONT of them, and fading a layer out near the eye leaves translucent ghosts hanging over that geometry | The backdrop draws behind every opaque mesh, and an alpha fade of a layer shows the geometry through half-seen leaves | Evaluate the same layer function in the geometry's own shader wherever its view ray crosses the layer's plane before reaching the fragment (eye below the plane, fragment above it), along the same ray and through the same air (hand the geometry's own in-scatter on rather than recomputing it), so the cover over it is the very pixel painted beside it; let the cover follow the layer's underside (lower toward a cluster's heart) so the geometry never ends at a level line. Fade a layer near the eye by THINNING it (cut its signed distance until the clusters drop out whole), never by alpha. Write the layers as one TSL `Loop` with per-layer constants picked by the index: two copies in JavaScript put the trunk shader 40 KB over budget. Precedent: `rbStoreys` in `src/themes/rainbark/rainbark-tsl.js` (2026-10-21) |
| #92 | A camera-facing ribbon built in WORLD space (`side = cross(axis, toCamera)`) draws nothing, with no error | Its winding faces away from the camera and the default `FrontSide` culls it. Quads built in clip space keep the plane's winding and are unaffected | `material.side = THREE.DoubleSide` |
| #211 | A torn or frayed edge cut with `discard` (a wave's lip, a cloth's fringe) shows a screen door of single pixels, and fingers of the tear read as icicles | The last of the edge was thinned by a hash of the SCREEN pixel, on a single-sampled pass; and the tear's noise was drawn out far along the edge's direction | Write the edge as the material's alpha from a fine noise of the SURFACE (it stays on the object as it moves), cut it with `alphaTest = 0.5` + `alphaToCoverage = true` (three ramps it over `fwidth`, so MSAA gives a coverage edge and a single-sampled pass a plain test), give the scene pass `sceneSamples()`, and draw the tear out no more than about 3:1. Precedent: the lip in `waves-water.js` (2026-10-21) |
