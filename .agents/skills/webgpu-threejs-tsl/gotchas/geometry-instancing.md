# Geometry, instancing and vertex data

Read before writing a `positionNode` or `vertexNode`, instancing anything, merging or generating a mesh in code, or loading a glTF; and when instances collapse, a mesh never draws on WebGPU, or a generated mesh shades flat or dull.

**House pattern for many small things.** `InstancedBufferGeometry` with ONE `InstancedInterleavedBuffer` per pool (WebGPU binds at most eight vertex buffers), integer-coded per-instance data, closed-form motion in the vertex stage, every pool always drawn with dormant slots at zero size.

One gotcha per line; the number is the row's permanent id (cited elsewhere as "gotcha #NN"). Rows numbered below 300 were numbered before these files existed and keep their numbers, in no order. A NEW row goes at the END of this table and takes the next free number in **700–799**. To search: `node .claude/skills/webgpu-threejs-tsl/scripts/gotcha.mjs <words>` (`README.md` in this folder).

| #65 | Instanced mask/displacement lands in wrong space, or instancing collapses to one transform | r185 **inverted** `positionNode` ordering (verified from generated WGSL): r181 ran `positionNode` first (`positionLocal` inside it = raw geometry) and applied instance/batch/skin matrices *after* its output; r185 applies the matrices *first* (`positionLocal` is post-instance inside `positionNode`) and the node's output is **final** | Portable idiom: compute masks/pivots/phases from `positionGeometry`, output `positionLocal.add(displacement)`. Never output a `positionGeometry`-only expression on a real-matrix InstancedMesh — on r185 that discards the instance matrices |
| #66 | Instanced/point particles invisible or collapsed to a dot | `positionNode = buffer.element(instanceIndex)` replaces *every vertex* with one point | `positionLocal.add(buffer.element(instanceIndex))`, or the `vertexNode` billboard pattern in `src/themes/winter/winter-snowfall.js` |
| #99 | `Render pipeline creation failed (...): Vertex buffer count (9) exceeds the maximum number of vertex buffers (8)`, the mesh never draws on WebGPU (the WebGL2 backend draws it) | On WebGPU every `BufferAttribute` is its own vertex buffer and a pipeline may bind eight. `position` + `uv` + seven instanced attributes is nine | Pack the per-instance attributes into `InstancedInterleavedBuffer`s (one for what never changes, one `DynamicDrawUsage` for what gameplay writes) and expose them as `InterleavedBufferAttribute`s under the same names; `attribute('aName', 'vec4')` reads them unchanged, and several draws can share the buffers. | #161 | `BufferGeometryUtils.mergeGeometries` returns `null` ("make sure index attribute exists among all geometries, or in none of them") and the merged mesh is missing | `ExtrudeGeometry` and the `PolyhedronGeometry` family (icosahedron, …) are non-indexed; `BoxGeometry`, `SphereGeometry`, `CylinderGeometry`, `LatheGeometry`, `PlaneGeometry` are indexed. The merge needs an index on all or none, and identical attribute names (Extrude also carries `uv`) | Give each non-indexed piece a trivial index (`0…n−1`, `Uint32Array` above 65 535 vertices) and strip every piece to one attribute set before merging. Same rebuild, `ensureAttrs` in `src/themes/rainbow-terraces/rainbow-terraces-isles.js` |
| #163 | Rocks grown from `IcosahedronGeometry` (displaced by noise) shade faceted however high the detail | The polyhedron family is non-indexed: every face has its own three vertices, so `computeVertexNormals` gives flat normals | Delete `normal` and `uv`, weld with `BufferGeometryUtils.mergeVertices(geo, 1e-5)`, then displace and `computeVertexNormals`. Rainbow Terraces' crags, trees and far isles (`icosphere` in `rainbow-terraces-isles.js`) |
| #118 | A generated mesh is dull, never catches the key light, shows no specular — but nothing is missing and nothing errors (a fish's back darker than its fins, flat dark lily pads) | Its triangles are wound the wrong way, so `computeVertexNormals()` gave normals that point INTO the surface. With `side: DoubleSide` (or an unlit material that reads its own `varying` normal, which three does not flip for back faces) the mesh still draws, lit from behind: `dot(N, L)` is negative on the faces you see | Check the facing numerically when you build a mesh in code — the mean of `normal.y` over a surface that should face up, or `sign(normal.z) === sign(position.z)` on a body's flanks — and pin it in a unit test (`tests/unit/koi-pond-meshes.test.js`). Swap two indices per triangle to fix. Found on Koi Pond's koi and lily pads (2026-10-08): both were lit by ambient light alone until a close-up showed fins brighter than bodies |
| #249 | A generated tube's end piece (a maw, a lip, a cap) shows its INSIDE material from outside (teeth on a closed dome) and its outside where the eye looks in, though the body's inside/outside test (`frontFacing`) is right | The piece's rings were appended running the other way (rim → apex while the body runs head → tail), so its triangles wind the other way and its front faces are its inside | Flip the test on that piece: `inside = select(frontFacing, isCap, 1 - isCap)` with the piece's flag as a per-vertex attribute, and capture the piece closed AND open. | #97 | `THREE.TSL: 'dFdx' is not supported in the vertex stage` on a loaded glTF | The model ships without normals (the Khronos sample fox does), so `normalWorld` falls back to screen-space derivatives; a `varying(...)` that contains it evaluates them in the vertex stage | Set `material.flatShading = true` after construction (it is not a constructor option of `MeshBasicNodeMaterial`) and keep the normal out of vertex-stage expressions. | #159 | Tetromino pieces built as four rounded cubes read as "four bits" (grooves and seams between the cells), not as the game's solid pieces; the user asked for them back as solid colour | Each cell was its own rounded box, so every shared face left a groove and a crease in the normals | Build each shape ONCE as a fused solid: trace the polyomino's outline (shared edges cancel), inset it by the bevel size and round its corners, extrude with `ExtrudeGeometry` (`bevelSize = bevelThickness`, depth `1 − 2·bevel`), delete `uv`/`normal`, `mergeVertices`, `computeVertexNormals`. Seven instanced meshes share one material; a locked piece's cells are matched to (shape, quarter turns) and the turn goes in its quaternion. Shade as polished solid colour (own colour lit by a key and a top light, a light of its own, clear-coat Fresnel, a glint); refraction, if any, only as a minority term. Same rebuild, `createShapeGeometry` / `matchShape` in `serenity-warp-glass.js` |
| #700 | Need many small meshes of DIFFERENT shapes on screen at once (a fleet: a saucer, a dart, a flying wing, a barge), any mix, a couple of dozen, without a pipeline per shape | A mesh per shape is a pipeline per shape per pass (the scene and the lagoon's mirror), all built at start for things that fly a few seconds a minute; a single mesh with `setDrawRange` draws one shape at a time | One buffer with every shape in it and its shape id on each vertex, drawn as ONE instanced mesh: each instance reads where it is, its quaternion and its shape id from a `uniformArray` of vec4 rows by `instanceIndex.mul(ROWS).add(k)`, and the material's `vertexNode` is `mix(vec4(0, 0, 2, 1), clip, step(abs(shapeOfVertex.sub(shapeOfInstance)), 0.5))`: a vertex of another shape leaves the clip volume, so its triangles cost a vertex shader and no pixel. A custom `vertexNode` sets none of three's own varyings: carry the world position and the turned normal yourself (`varying(world)`, `varying(turn(normal))`) and never read `positionWorld` or `normalWorld` in that material. Set `geometry.instanceCount` to the number alive each frame, and hide the mesh when it is none. `createHullMaterial` and `createVisitors` in `src/themes/halcyon-apex/halcyon-apex-visitors.js` (2026-11-16): eight hulls, 7,765 vertices, 24 slots, one pipeline. |
| #701 | Need several different models in ONE instanced mesh (asteroids, rocks, debris) without a mesh, and so a pipeline, per model | — | Merge the models into one geometry with a per-vertex model id, give each instance the id it wears, and fold every other model's vertices onto the instance's middle in the vertex stage (`wears = step(abs(modelId − look.x), 0.5)`, position times `wears`): degenerate triangles are culled, so only vertex work is paid for. Budget the SUM of all models' vertices per instance and per class (six models of 640 triangles on 44 boulders is fine; a class of 6,000 gravel keeps one or two models of under 40, the more so when its vertex stage evaluates an event table). Add a per-instance stretch on three axes for free variety, and keep the instance attributes within the eight vertex buffers (#99). Baked smooth normals on a low-poly rock read as clay: blend toward the face normal from `cross(dFdx(world), dFdy(world))`, sign-aligned to the smooth one, for facets without split vertices, and keep the smooth normal for rim light. A decimated level stands a few per cent outside the fine mesh's bound: normalise a design once over all its levels. `createStoneGeometry` and `createRocks` in `src/themes/void-ember/void-ember-belt.js`, models from `scripts/blender/void_ember_stones.py` (2026-11-16) |
| #702 | A generated solid (a crystal, a cut stone: any mesh whose triangles you list yourself) looks right in every capture, silhouette clean, facets lit and glinting, yet the light on each face falls from the wrong side, mirrored left for right, and a close look shows the far walls, not the near ones | The triangles are wound clockwise seen from outside. The usual way in: the corner order was kept while the frame a ring of corners is built in changed hand (`W = A × U` instead of `U × A` turns the ring the other way round). With the default `FrontSide` the near faces are culled as back faces and each solid is drawn from inside: its far walls, whose normals (`computeVertexNormals` follows the winding) point back at the eye, so they shade plausibly. A convex solid's silhouette does not change, so nothing looks broken | Test the winding, do not judge it by eye: for every triangle `(b − a) × (c − a)` must point away from its own solid's middle (away from the axis for a prism side, from each small part's own centre for beads and caps), for every tier's plan. List corners counter-clockwise from outside; when you change the frame's hand, change the order too. Precedent: `buildSpiresGeometry` in `src/themes/vesper-chrysalis/vesper-chrysalis-shore.js` and its test "cuts every crystal with its faces looking outward" in `tests/unit/vesper-chrysalis-meshes.test.js` |

| #704 | Foreground things that belong to a level of a climbing camera (sprays hanging into the top of the frame, wrapped up by one storey as the camera rises) jump into view mid-climb on a tall phone frame, though the same wrap is invisible at 16:9 | The wrap can hide only when the lens sees less than one period of height at the thing's depth, and how much it sees depends on the frame. At a depth d the frame is `(tan(fov/2 + pitch) + tan(fov/2 − pitch)) · d` tall: 0.94 d on the 16:9 rest lens, 1.29 d upright. A spray 8 m off plus its own length fits under a 12 m storey on one and not on the other. Rounding to the nearest storey also wraps everything at the same instant, half-way up, when most of it is in the middle of the frame | Hang by the lens, not by metres: scale each thing's depth by the reference lens's band over the live one and its rise by the top-edge slopes, so every frame sees the same share of a storey (`sprayPlace`, `lensEdges` in `src/themes/rainbark/rainbark-core.js`; the uniform `u.lens`). | #709 | Need a wood that mixes forty kinds and ages of tree without a draw for each (an `InstancedMesh` has one geometry, so variety was a bucket per species per tier: 44 draws for seven species) | A vertex shader does not have to read a shape from vertex attributes: it can PULL it. | Lay every model out in one half-float `DataTexture` (a card is four texels, a bark vertex two) and give each instance the texel its model starts at and how many cards and vertices it has. | #710 | Need the picture's centre somewhere other than the middle of the screen (a UI panel covers one side of it); or: after a `camera.setViewOffset` every sphere is an ellipse and the whole frame looks squeezed | `PerspectiveCamera.setViewOffset(fullWidth, fullHeight, x, y, width, height)` writes `camera.aspect = fullWidth / fullHeight`. Called with the frame as 1 x 1 (offsets as shares of the screen) it squares the lens: a 16:9 picture drawn through a 1:1 frustum. Nothing warns, every later `updateProjectionMatrix()` keeps it, and a resize that sets `camera.aspect` hides it again until the next shift | Shift the LENS, not the camera: give the full frame as aspect x 1, `camera.setViewOffset(a, 1, -sx * a, sy, a, 1)` with `a = camera.aspect` and `sx`, `sy` the shares of the screen the picture's centre moves (right, up). The eye and its aim stay put, and raycasts (`Raycaster.setFromCamera`), `Vector3.project` and every node that reads `cameraProjectionMatrix` follow by themselves; only code that rebuilds view rays from `fov` and `aspect` by hand would not.
