# Numerics: NaN, overflow, precision and hashing

Read when a frame or a region goes black with no console error, a pattern sparkles or flickers per pixel, something breaks only in portrait or only after an hour of play, and before writing any `pow`, `exp`, `normalize`, `hash` or `floor` on values you do not control.

**Why one bad pixel matters here.** Every theme runs bloom, and a bloom mip chain spreads a single NaN or Inf texel over the whole frame. So "bounded" is a property each term needs before it is masked, mixed or summed: a mask does not protect against Inf (`Inf * 0` is NaN). Hash and `floor` inputs need the same care in the other direction: they amplify the last bit, so their inputs must be exact (small integers, non-negative).

One gotcha per line; the number is the row's permanent id (cited elsewhere as "gotcha #NN"). Rows numbered below 300 were numbered before these files existed and keep their numbers, in no order. A NEW row goes at the END of this table and takes the next free number in **500–599**. To search: `node .claude/skills/webgpu-threejs-tsl/scripts/gotcha.mjs <words>` (`README.md` in this folder).

| # | Symptom | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|---|
| #94 | Whole frame goes **black** (no console error) as soon as one material is on screen; a tighter camera angle renders fine | One fragment produced **NaN** and the bloom mip chain spread it over the image. Usual source: `pow(x, n)` with a base a hair below zero — Schlick's `pow(1 - cos, 5)` when a normalised dot product returns 1.0000001, or `sqrt`/`pow` after a subtraction that should be >= 0. `pow2/pow3/pow4` are plain multiplies and are safe | Clamp the cosine (`clamp(dot(...), 0, 1)`) and write small integer powers as products (`m.mul(m)`…). Bisect by hiding one material at a time: the frame returns when the culprit leaves. Precedent: `schlick()` in `src/themes/crystal-cave/crystal-cave-gems.js` |
| #71 | A region goes BLACK behind a mask that should hide a term (sky vanishes above a ridge, a disc eats its backdrop) — often ONLY on a tall portrait screen | The masked-out term is unbounded there: a rim light written `exp(k * signedDistance)` reaches 1e8 on the far side, and `mix(a, b, 1)` evaluated as `a + (b - a)` loses `b` entirely to float cancellation. A 390 × 844 screen spans more than twice the hero-space height of 16:9, so an overflow that stayed at the float maximum (and was masked away) in landscape becomes Inf, then NaN | Bound every term before it is masked: `exp(max(distance, 0).mul(-k))`, and capture portrait before calling it done. Found on the breathing Aurora world (2026-10-05), `src/ui/effects/breathing/worlds/aurora.js`; the portrait form on the forest's fern rim, `worlds/forest.js` |
| #231 | The whole frame goes black for a moment when two light sources meet (a conjunction, an eclipse), with no error; the sky alone shows it with the post off | Something drawn between the two sources normalises the vector between them (a ribbon's chord, a bridge's axis, a cross product of the two directions): when they coincide it is `normalize(0)` = NaN, and multiplying the term by a zero strength does not help (NaN × 0 = NaN); bloom then spreads the NaN over the frame | Add a tiny fixed offset before every such normalise (`normalize(B − A + vec3(1e-5, 2e-5, 0))`, `normalize(cross(m, a) + vec3(0, 1e-6, 0))`) and capture the moment the sources meet. Found on Sunbraid's braided ribbons (2026-10-20), `ribbonBetween` in `sunbraid-tsl.js` |
| #105 | Black, red and green dashes along thin bright strips (inlaid lines, rims, ribbons) once they are far away; clean up close and clean without MSAA | With MSAA a fragment's varyings are evaluated at the pixel centre even when only a corner sample is covered, so on a strip thinner than a pixel a UV is EXTRAPOLATED far outside 0..1. A profile such as `1 - abs(uv.x - 0.5) * 2` goes strongly negative, the colour goes negative, and fog or bloom then mixes it per channel | `clamp()` every interpolated coordinate (or the profile built from it) before it shapes a colour; the same for barycentrics and any per-vertex 0..1 ramp. Found on Halcyon Apex's ley lines (2026-10-08), `createLeyMaterial` in `src/themes/halcyon-apex/halcyon-apex-stone.js` |
| #87 | A hashed pattern sparkles per pixel inside one instance (speckled windows, dithered masks) | A per-instance attribute read in the fragment stage is an interpolated varying: a constant comes back with last-bit noise, and a hash amplifies that into a different value per pixel. A large hash input (a seed in the thousands times a fraction) loses the same bits on its own | Store instance seeds as INTEGERS, `floor(seed.add(0.5))` in the shader, and split a big seed into two small integers (`mod(seed, 64)`, `floor(seed / 64)`) so every hash input is exactly representable. Found on Neon District's facades (2026-10-05), `neon-district-facades.js` |
| #204 | Hashed cells (Voronoi points, per-cell jitter) fall on a regular lattice over part of the pattern, irregular elsewhere | TSL's `hash(seed)` converts the seed with `toUint()`; WGSL clamps a negative float to 0 there (GLSL leaves it undefined), so every cell whose seed is negative hashes the same. A seed built from signed cell coordinates (`c.x * 127.1 + c.y * 311.7`) loses half its cells this way | Shift the cell coordinates positive and hash an exact integer: `c = floor(p).add(256)`, `hash(c.x.add(c.y.mul(1031)))`. One hash a cell is enough: `fract(h.mul(64))` gives a second coordinate. Found on the Odyssey forest's leaf clumps (2026-10-26), `fvLeaf` in `src/rendering/odyssey/world/odyssey-world-renderer.js` |
| #88 | A whole row or column of a procedural grid flickers between present and absent per pixel | `floor()` of a quotient of interpolated per-instance values that divides EXACTLY (storeys = (height - plinth) / storey height) lands either side of the integer from pixel to pixel | Bias the quotient before the floor (`floor(q.add(0.02))`) wherever the layout can produce an exact division. `src/themes/neon-district/neon-district-facades.js` |
| #174 | Event effects (blooms, fills, rosettes) vanish or freeze after about an hour of play, never in captures | The world writes absolute times into the event rows while the shaders compare them with a wrapped clock (`u.time = phase`, periodic) | Write every timestamp the shaders read on the wrapped clock: `t − (time − phase)`; ages stay right across the wrap |
