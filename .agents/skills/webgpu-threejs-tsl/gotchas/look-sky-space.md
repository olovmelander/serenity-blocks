# Look: skies, stars, suns, space bodies and lenses

Read before building or changing a sky function, a star field, a sun or moon disc, a planet, ring, corona, nebula shell, black hole, wormhole, tunnel or rainbow.

**The recurring lessons.** Lay stars out on a 3D lattice of the direction, never in longitude and latitude; size things for a reference frame and floor them at a buffer pixel; draw round things in the lens's image plane; and light a thin shell by the length of the ray inside it, faded before its edge.

One gotcha per line; the number is the row's permanent id (cited elsewhere as "gotcha #NN"). Rows numbered below 300 were numbered before these files existed and keep their numbers, in no order. A NEW row goes at the END of this table and takes the next free number in **1400–1499**. To search: `node .claude/skills/webgpu-threejs-tsl/scripts/gotcha.mjs <words>` (`README.md` in this folder).

|---|---|---|---|
| #148 | Stars come out as short streaks that all point at one place in the sky (often just above the frame), though each is drawn as a round Gaussian | The stars were laid out in cells of (longitude, latitude), or of bearing and height in a rotated frame: near that frame's pole a cell is narrow across the meridian, so a spot that is round in cell units is stretched along it by about 1/cos². Turning the sky (a star clock) brings that pole into view | Lay the cells out on a 3D lattice instead: `cell = floor(q · scale)`, one star a cell at `normalize(cell + hash3(cell) · 0.56 + 0.22)`, the spot from `\|q − star\| · scale`. It is the same everywhere on the sphere and has no seam; a star whose cell the sphere barely crosses is simply missing. Found on Sunset (2026-10-11), `starLayer` in `src/themes/sunset/sunset-tsl.js` |
| #146 | A procedural Milky Way reads as smoke, plaster or torn paper, however the noise is tuned | The band was built only from reads of a noise texture: magnified they are soft cloud, thresholded fine octaves are pepper, and a read stretched 3:1 along the band turns the fine octave into dashes | Let noise do only the broad things (star clouds, dust lanes read at mip 1 to 2 with a wide smoothstep, UV stretch under 2:1), and make the band out of POINTS: a grid of cells in the galaxy's own coordinates (longitude, latitude), at most one Gaussian point a cell at a hashed place, present where a hash is under the local density. The points are analytic, so they neither blur under magnification nor crawl as the sky turns. Give the catalogue's instanced stars the same density on the CPU (the dust function reads the same baked noise), so the stars thin out where the lanes are. Same rebuild, `src/themes/starlight/starlight-sky.js`, `starlight-core.js` |
| #147 | On a phone's small buffer (pixel ratio 0.5) every star is a blob and a fireball is a white cone; on a 4K buffer the same stars are specks | Sizes were set in pixels of whatever buffer is drawn (`pixels × angle of one buffer pixel`), so a thing's share of the sky follows the resolution | Author sizes for one reference frame and floor them at about a buffer pixel: `max(design × pixels, texel × least)`, with `design` = the angle of a pixel of a 1080-line frame and `texel` = the angle of a real buffer pixel (anti-aliasing widths use `texel` alone). And a streak whose strip ends flat at its bright end reads as an arrowhead at any size: round the nose (width × √(1 − t²) over the last tenth) and space the strip's rows closer toward the head. The phone validator's SCREENSHOTS showed both; its verdict was "pass". Same rebuild, `sized()` in `src/themes/starlight/starlight-tsl.js`, `starlight-fx.js` |
| #151 | A procedural star lattice (stars hashed per cell of a direction) turns into television static when the view direction is remapped (relativistic aberration, a fisheye, a zoom) | The remap squeezes many cells into each pixel, so every pixel holds a star; brightening them "because light piles up ahead" makes it worse | Fade the lattice out with the remap's strength and let instanced stars carry the motion; of those, dim the ones whose trail on screen is under a few pixels (`smoothstep(2, 16, trailLengthPx)`), so only stars near enough to streak are seen at speed. Same rebuild: `backdropFn` in `stellar-velocity-nebula.js`, `atSpeed` in `stellar-velocity-stars.js` |
| #145 | Star trails (a long exposure) are wanted, but a feedback buffer that accumulates frames cannot be replayed from a seek, smears everything else in the pass and needs a ping-pong target on both backends | Trails were thought of as an image effect | Make the trail a property of the star: keep every star in polar coordinates about the pole (cos ρ, sin ρ, θ) and draw it as an instanced STRIP laid along its own circle, from `θ + skyAngle` back by an `exposure` uniform; shade the strip as a capsule in the fragment (distance to the clamped arc coordinate), with a bright point at the head. With the exposure at 0 the strip is one round point, so draw the same instances with a one-quad geometry until it opens: same material and the same attribute layout share the pipeline (`RenderObject.getGeometryCacheKey` is structural), so the switch costs no compile. Starlight rebuild 2026-10, `src/themes/starlight/starlight-stars.js` |
| #153 | A disc drawn in direction space (a sun, a moon, a planet: gnomonic coordinates about its own direction) is round in the middle of the frame and an upright ellipse near its edge, worst on a phone's wide portrait lens | The disc is a circle on the SKY, and a rectilinear lens stretches whatever lies far off its axis (a disc 35° off-axis comes out half as tall again) | Draw it in the lens's IMAGE PLANE: `p = (dot(d, camRight), dot(d, camUp)) / dot(d, camFwd) − centre`, over the disc's radius in tangent units, with `centre` computed on the CPU from the disc's world direction every frame (so it still follows a drifting or shaking camera). One buffer pixel is then `texel / tanR` disc units everywhere, so anti-aliasing widths are exact. Precedent: `sunPlane` in `src/themes/solar-eclipse/solar-eclipse-tsl.js` (2026-10-11). When a `reflector()` also draws the disc, build the image plane from the camera NODES instead (`cameraViewMatrix.mul(vec4(d, 0))`, `xy / −z`, centre from the same transform of the disc's direction) so the mirror camera measures its own circle, and multiply the face's vertical coordinate by `sign(cameraViewMatrix · (0, 1, 0, 0)).y`: the reflector rolls its camera upside down, and without it the mirrored face is drawn inverted. `ndSunDisc` in `src/themes/neon-dusk/neon-dusk-tsl.js` (2026-10-21) |
| #154 | A solar corona built from noise reads as smoke, marble or a cotton ball with contour lines, never as the fine rays of an eclipse photograph | Rays were noise in polar coordinates, warped by radius; or the closed field was one evenly bright region with a lit boundary, which draws an outlined bubble | Read the rays along a dipole's own field-line label: a point at radius r and latitude λ lies on the line whose foot is `acos(cos λ / √min(r, Rss))`; below the cusp `acos(1/√Rss)` the lines close (helmet arches, rays nearly smooth there), above the source surface they run straight out. A broad warp of the label may vary with `min(r, Rss)` only, never with r beyond it (that is what turns rays to smoke). Make the helmets two bulbs on the equator (`exp(−(sin λ / 0.45)²)` inside the closed field) and give the streamer stalks their own slow fall-off (`r^−1.55`) so the wings reach out. Read north and south of the current sheet from two rows of the noise, blended across it, or the label's sign flip draws a seam. Precedent: the corona in `src/themes/solar-eclipse/solar-eclipse-sun.js` |
| #142 | Need exact two-way shadows between a globe and a thin ring (the ring's shadow on the cloud tops, the globe's shadow on the ring), a ring that is translucent by its own thickness, and clean edges on every tier without MSAA | — | Trace them in ONE full-screen pass (`vertexNode = vec4(positionGeometry.xy * 2, 0.5, 1)`, the view ray built from camera-basis uniforms): a ray–sphere and a ray–plane hit per pixel, composited along the ray (sky, far ring, globe, near ring). Draw the ring from a 1D profile `DataTexture` (height 1) of optical depth whose mip chain is averaged on the CPU (`tex.mipmaps = levels`, `generateMipmaps = false`) and read the SAME profile for the shadow on the globe (`exp(-tau / sin(sun elevation))` at the radius where the surface point's sun ray crosses the plane), so every gap is a line of light in the shadow. Choose the mip yourself from the pixel's footprint (`.level(log2(footprint / texel))`): the fetch sits inside a branch. Anti-alias the limb from the ray's closest approach in pixels, `clamp(0.5 - (b - R) / pixelSize)`. Geometry drawn on top (ice, meteors) tests itself against the sphere in its vertex stage. Both backends. Precedent: `src/themes/stellar-drift/stellar-drift-space.js` (2026-10-11) |
| #143 | A palette change on a banded surface (a gas giant, strata, a striped sky) is a flat global cross-fade, and half-way it is mud | Every band blends between the two palettes at the same moment | Keep the bands' colours in one table texture with a ROW per palette (sRGB `DataTexture`, `wrapT = RepeatWrapping`, no mips) and read it at `v = (floor(place) + 0.5 + smoothstep(when, when + 0.34, fract(place))) / rows`, where `when` comes from the band's own seed: the sampler's bilinear filter blends two neighbouring rows, each band at its own moment, so the change crosses the surface like weather and no band is ever a mix of unrelated hues; the wrap closes the wheel from the last palette to the first. Where a band is thinner than a pixel (the limb) mix toward a second, narrow table that holds the same rows blurred: a table with one row per palette cannot be mipped (the rows would blend). Precedent: `bandV` in `stellar-drift-space.js`, `bakeBandColors` in `stellar-drift-bake.js` |
| #131 | Filaments drawn from noise on a sphere (or any surface) read as scribbles: short squiggles that close on themselves, all one width | The threads are where an isotropic field crosses its middle, and those crossings are closed loops the size of the noise's cells | Read the pattern from the surface SQUEEZED along an axis (`p − axis · dot(p, axis) · 0.45`, a different axis per layer) so the crossings run on as long strands, warp the fine read by the coarse one, and let a second field switch each thread on and off along its length. Draw the thread itself as `(1 − \|2n − 1\|)^k` on a SMOOTH low-resolution field: its sharpness then comes from the shader, not from the texture. `src/themes/supernova/supernova-nebula.js` |
| #130 | A shock front, bubble wall or any thin shell fills the frame as a flat translucent disc with a hard edge, and several at once wash the picture out | The shell was shaded like a textured surface: a density at the two points where the view ray crosses a sphere. An even density gives every ray through the disc the same light, so the shell is a filled circle, and its only edge is where the rays stop meeting it | Give the shell a thickness and solve the length of the ray inside it: with `d` the ray's closest approach to the centre, `run = (√(R² − d²) − √(r² − d²)) / √(R² − r²)` is 1 where the ray grazes the inner face and 0 at the outer one. Cubed, it is a ring with a crisp outer edge, a soft inner side and next to nothing across its face, and it needs no noise read at all. Found on Supernova's shock fronts (2026-10-09), `src/themes/supernova/supernova-nebula.js` |
| #222 | A thin glowing shell (a nebula's bubble, a shock's skin, a planet's atmosphere drawn as one sphere) reads as a glass bubble: a hard bright line traces its outline | The light was weighted by the ray's path length through the skin (`skin / √(cos² + skin²)`), which peaks exactly where the ray stops meeting the sphere: the brightest band IS the edge, and nothing lies beyond it | Fade the graze over the last stretch of the cosine, `graze × smoothstep(0, 1.3 × skin, cos)`, so the limb-brightened band rides just inside the edge and the edge itself dissolves. Found on Supernova's nebula (2026-10-21), `supernova-nebula.js` |
| #256 | A ring of light, shockwave or halo drawn round a sphere (a planet, a star) reads as a glass bubble or a force field: a crisp circle traces its outline, and once it grows past a radius or two it crosses the frame as another planet's limb | The shell was lit by the ray's run through a hard-edged slab (the run-length form peaks exactly at the inner face), and it was allowed to grow as far as the event was strong, though the sphere is already a third of the screen | Give the shell a Gaussian profile of width w about radius R and integrate it along the ray in closed form: inside (closest approach d < R) `√(2Rw / (R² − d² + 2Rw))`, outside `exp(−((d − R)/3w)²)` (a slower tail outside, so there is no rim). It is brightest just off the limb with no hard line at either edge. Tear it into radial filaments with a ridged 3D noise of the closest-approach direction, keep it within about 1.6 radii, show 15 % of it over the disc, and let a wider front run on in the ring plane separately. Found on Stellar Drift's corona (2026-10-21), the flare at the end of `src/themes/stellar-drift/stellar-drift-space.js` |
| #158 | A wormhole, portal or lens drawn in a sky function shows snow in its throat, a seam at its rim, or a rim that is a soft blob | The other side was mapped into the disc without accounting for how hard the map squeezes it, and the two sides were switched with a hard `θ < α` | Inside, fold the other sky's whole sphere into the disc (`γ = π (θ/α)^p`, p ≈ 1.35 magnifies the middle); outside, bend ours by the point-lens law `β = θ − α²/θ` (whatever stands behind the throat becomes the Einstein ring). Scale every star lattice's footprint by the map's derivative (outside `1 + α²/θ²`, inside `π p (θ/α)^(p−1) / α`) and dim it by `(spread / footprint)²`, blend the two sides over one pixel of footprint, and give the rim ring an exponent that never resolves below a pixel. Keep a feature that must stay a point on screen (the far galaxy's nucleus) in SCREEN angle, not in the folded coordinate, or it swells to a disc as the throat opens. Found on Serenity Warp (2026-10-11), `skyLight` in `src/themes/serenity-warp/serenity-warp-sky.js` |
| #235 | A lensed sky (a black hole, a wormhole) shows concentric ribs or record grooves round the Einstein ring | Its grain was read from an anisotropic 2D tile (long features) through equirectangular coordinates: the lens wraps those coordinates round the ring, so the tile's lanes become rings | Read sky grain and clouds as 3D noise along the bent ray's direction (the Void Ember lattice: `bakeLattice`, one fetch, two fields), which has no poles and no seam, and ease fine grain and nebula contrast off where the lens stretches hardest (`smoothstep` on the impact parameter, about 1.4 × the Einstein radius). `sky()` in `src/themes/black-hole/black-hole-lens.js` |
| #234 | A volume drawn round a black hole fills the shadow with a haze, or with a blue sheen right at the horizon; or the frame goes black once the volume is on | The rays that fall in skim the densest, most Doppler-beamed gas just over the inner disk, and a temperature-weighted colour there runs off the blue end. An orbital speed `√(0.5 / (r − 1))` taken at a step that passes over the axis exceeds 1, and `√(1 − v²)` is NaN | Keep a few per cent of the volume's light on rays the march captures (`haze *= mix(0.04, 1, escaped)`), fade it out inside three radii, cap its temperature short of blue, and clamp the speed's radius (`max(r − 1, 0.62)` holds v < 0.9). `src/themes/black-hole/black-hole-lens.js` |
| #237 | Streaks in a spinning disc (an accretion disc, a whirlpool, a galaxy) read as a planet's fine concentric rings after a while, though they were drawn as broken streaks | Each radius turns at its own rate (Keplerian `ω ∝ r^−1.5`), so a read offset by `time · ω(r)` shears without limit: after tens of seconds every streak is wound into a hairline ring | Bound the winding the way a flow map is: two reads half a cycle apart, each offset by `fract(t / T + shift) · T · ω(r)` and weighted by a triangle `1 − \|2·phase − 1\|`, so each is faded out before its shear grows (T ≈ 6 s). | #226 | A kaleidoscope (polar mirror fold of the view ray) shows a straight colour seam running from the frame's centre, or doubles its hero object into a close pair | Two separate causes.
