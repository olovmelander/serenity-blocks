# Textures, noise, mips and render targets

Read before choosing a noise source, baking a texture, lighting a surface from a height or noise field, or drawing into a `RenderTarget` (feedback, paint, simulation); and when a pattern is serrated, faceted, blocky, lined at cell borders, upside down or empty.

**House defaults.** No `mx_noise_*` (compile time, #80). Noise is a 2D texture baked on the CPU at build (through `bakeOnce`, `src/themes/shared/bake-cache.js`), read at an explicit `.level()`; 3D noise is the 2D-lattice trick (#136). A filtered texture is continuous in value but not in slope, so a normal comes from a baked gradient, never from differences of a magnified read.

One gotcha per line; the number is the row's permanent id (cited elsewhere as "gotcha #NN"). Rows numbered below 300 were numbered before these files existed and keep their numbers, in no order. A NEW row goes at the END of this table and takes the next free number in **1000–1099**. To search: `node .claude/skills/webgpu-threejs-tsl/scripts/gotcha.mjs <words>` (`README.md` in this folder).

|---|---|---|---|
| #136 | Need 3D noise per pixel on a curved or volumetric surface (a star, a corona, stones) on both backends, without `mx_noise_*`, without a 3D texture and without three fetches per sample | — | A 256² RGBA8 lattice of white noise whose G channel is R shifted by one z-slice (+37, +17 texels) and whose A is B shifted the same way: `uv = (floor(p.xy) + vec2(37, 17) * floor(p.z) + smooth(fract(p.xy)) + 0.5) / 256`, one bilinear fetch at `.level(0)`, then `mix(t.xz, t.yw, smooth(fract(p.z)))` is trilinear value noise for two independent fields at once. Repeat wrapping, no mips, coordinates in lattice cells. Octaves are separate fetches at different scales; take ridges for cells and cracks. Precedent: `bakeLattice` and `u.noise3` in `src/themes/void-ember/void-ember-tsl.js` |
| #132 | A surface pattern must change shape over time (a boiling star, churning cloud) and there is only a 3D noise texture, no 4D noise | Scrolling the read slides the pattern across the surface; it does not change it | Slide the whole surface through the volume: add one offset to every read (`q · f + (0, 0, t · k)` for points `q` of a sphere). Each point then sees a different slice, so cells swell, split and merge. For flow ALONG a direction (streamers running outward) the read has to move along that direction, which cannot go on for ever: take two reads half a cycle apart and cross-fade them, each fading out as it reaches the end of its run. Precedent: the photosphere and `streamAt` in `src/themes/supernova/supernova-star.js` |
| #133 | Fine noise detail shimmers or crawls when the camera drifts, on a texture without mips (a `Data3DTexture`, a baked field) | Where a texel of the read is smaller than a pixel the read aliases, and without a mip chain nothing averages it | Fade the fine read out by its own footprint and skip it when it is gone: texels per pixel = pixel size on the surface × read frequency × texture size; `room = 1 − smoothstep(0.5, 1.1, texelsPerPixel)`, `If(room > 0.01, …)`. The detail then appears exactly where there is room to show it (a close-up, a 4K screen, the large outer layers). Precedent: `room` in `supernova-star.js` and `supernova-nebula.js` |
| #104 | An edge or a screen-space offset driven by the baked fBm texture comes out **serrated**: a fountain's silhouette saws instead of tearing in soft tongues, a heat haze zigzags instead of shimmering | The noise is read at mip 0 with a large gain, so its top octaves (64 cycles a tile) ride on the slow ones the effect wanted. Automatic mip selection does not help: the UVs change slowly, so the sampler picks level 0 | Ask for a blurred level explicitly: `texture(noise, uv).level(2.0)` (draws on WebGPU and on the WebGL2 backend). Found on Cinder Drift's fountains and heat haze (2026-10-08), `src/themes/cinder-drift/cinder-drift-falls.js`, `cinder-drift-post.js` |
| #268 | Dust, lanes or any sharpened read of a baked noise texture shows facets: straight edges and corners at the texel grid's scale, a field of cotton balls or cut paper rather than smoke | Bilinear filtering is continuous in value but piecewise linear, so a `smoothstep` (or any steep curve) of a magnified read draws the texel grid's diamonds; reading a coarser mip only makes them bigger | Ease the texel weights in the shader at an explicit whole mip, one fetch: `p = uv·size + 0.5`, `i = floor(p)`, `f = fract(p)`, `uv' = (i + f³(f(6f − 15) + 10) − 0.5) / size` with `size = N / 2^level`. Thresholded reads then draw curves. Prefer an extinction model to a threshold anyway (`light · exp(−τ)` with τ built from wide ramps), and give a CPU mirror the same reader and the same mip chain (2 × 2 means). Starlight's second pass (2026-10-21), `noiseSmooth` in `src/themes/starlight/starlight-tsl.js`, `sampleNoiseSmooth` / `noiseLevels` / `dustTau` in `starlight-core.js` |
| #98 | A thin line is ruled down every cell boundary of a glyph or sprite atlas | The cell index comes from `floor()`/`fract()` of a UV, so the atlas UV JUMPS between neighbouring pixels at each boundary; the automatic mip selection reads that jump as extreme minification and samples the coarsest level there, which holds the average of the whole atlas | Sample a fixed level (`texture(atlas, uv).level(1.0)`) or pass explicit gradients; keep the mip chain only for the level you choose. Found on Chiral Gold's combo tally (2026-10-05), `chiral-gold-fx.js` |
| #274 | A sprite or glyph atlas read with `.grad()` (explicit gradients) leaves the material undrawn on WebGPU (`unresolved value 'nodeUniformN_sampler'`; a Node build shows the read but no `var …_sampler : sampler`), or two atlases whose images are swapped in later both read the SAME image | The swappable `texture(placeholder)` started on a `DataTexture`, whose default `minFilter` and `magFilter` are `NearestFilter`: three counts it unfilterable and declares no sampler, yet `generateTextureGrad` still emits `textureSampleGrad(tex, tex_sampler, …)`. And three merges bindings by texture object: two nodes built on one placeholder are ONE binding, so when their `.value`s are later set to two images the shader still reads one of them | Give every swappable atlas a placeholder of its own, made with `LinearFilter` for both filters. Gate it in Node: every `nodeUniformN_sampler` a WGSL stage reads must be declared (`undeclaredBindings`), and count the texture reads you expect (`tests/unit/neon-district-shops-shaders.test.js`). Found on Neon District's people (2026-10-26), `this.people` in `src/themes/neon-district/neon-district-world.js` |
| #169 | Lighting a surface built from a baked noise texture (a cloud's billows, a cliff, a heightfield) with normals from finite differences shows square blocks and fine stripes that follow the noise, worst where a domain warp stretches it, though the colour read from the same texture is smooth | Bilinear filtering is continuous in value but not in slope: the gradient of a filtered texture steps at every texel edge, a warp read from another filtered texture adds its own steps through the chain rule, and a difference over a few thousandths of a unit amplifies both | Bake the exact gradient with the height (`R` = height, `G`/`B` = its analytic derivatives, computed on the CPU from the lattice gradients and the quintic fade's derivative) and read value and slope in one fetch; bilinear filtering of a gradient is continuous, so the normal is. Leave the slow warp out of the chain rule. Check the bake against a five-point stencil (correlation > 0.99). Found on Nebula Flow (2026-10-11), `bakeRelief` in `src/themes/nebula-flow/nebula-flow-core.js`, `dustAt` in `nebula-flow-nebula.js` |
| #246 | A bump from value noise's slope (its analytic derivative or finite differences of it) draws a grid of bricks or blocks in the light: square cells with straight edges, worst under a side or grazing light; the same noise scaling a shape's radius shows blocks in its outline | Value noise's fade has zero slope at every cell edge (`du(0) = du(1) = 0`), so its gradient dies along each lattice line, and a strong bump turns the lattice into a visible grid | Bump with a cellular field whose slope is smooth everywhere: one dome per jittered cell, joined by a smooth maximum (log-sum-exp), the exact gradient as the softmax weights' blend of each dome's own (`nvDomes` in `src/themes/nimbus-veil/nimbus-veil-tsl.js`). Keep value noise for values and for slow, weak slopes. Found on Nimbus Veil's towers (2026-10-21) |
| #272 | A surface lit from a baked fBm's exact gradient (dust, cloud, rock) shades as crumpled foil or creased clay: every lump is covered in fine dark creases, though the gradient is smooth and the silhouette looks right | Each octave of an fBm with persistence ½ halves in height but doubles in frequency, so every octave has the SAME slope: the normal carries all of them at equal weight, and the finest draw the light. Weighting the reads by scale does not help when each read is itself a whole fBm | Take the billows' slope from the same read at a coarser mip (`texture(relief, uv).level(footprintLod + 2.5).yz`: a box-filtered chain of a gradient is the gradient of the filtered height, so the top octaves drop out), let the full reads' slope crisp only the skin (weighted by nearness to the edge), and darken the folds by the relief's own height. One fetch. Judge it in a diffuse-only debug view: billows, not crumple. Found on Nebula Flow's atmosphere pass (2026-10-22), `billowSlope` in `src/themes/nebula-flow/nebula-flow-nebula.js` |
| #273 | Thin features drawn as a bump (ridges, cracks, grooves, seams) break into dotted, stair-stepped lines of 2 × 2 blocks, and lit stone or sand mottles in 2 × 2 squares; MSAA changes nothing | The normal came from screen derivatives of the height (`dFdx(h)`, `dFdy(h)` into the surface gradient). GPUs take derivatives once per 2 × 2 quad, so a profile a few pixels wide gets one slope per block, and a fine texture's slope jumps from quad to quad. MSAA only smooths triangle edges | Build the slope without screen derivatives of the height. For a profile across a distance field (a crack's distance `e` to its border, direction `n`), its mean slope over the pixel is `(h(abs(e − w/2)) − h(e + w/2)) / w`, with `w` the pixel's width across it (`abs(dot(dFdx(p), n)) + abs(dot(dFdy(p), n))`: derivatives of the smooth coordinate are fine); its colour box-filters the same way from the profile's integral. For a texture's relief, take two more reads a pixel's width away (mip-filtered alike). Where borders meet, take a smooth minimum over ALL of them (log-sum-exp, the gradient as the weights' blend of theirs): a minimum of the nearest two jumps where a third comes near, and a value drawn per border (a random height) jumps where three cells meet; both flickered at every junction in a temporal spike map. Found on Sunbraid's crust and towers (2026-10-25): `sbCellEdge` in `src/themes/sunbraid/sunbraid-tsl.js`, `sunbraid-land.js`, `sunbraid-spires.js` |
| #113 | Content drawn into a `RenderTarget` with a camera (a paint or feedback target) comes out upside down when the target is sampled; nothing errors, and a symmetric picture hides it | A render target's **v = 0 is its TOP row on both backends**: WebGPU stores it so, and `TextureNode.setupUV` flips WebGL's (`builder.isFlipY()`). `QuadMesh`'s own uv runs DOWN the screen (`QuadGeometry`: `[0, -1, 0, 1, 2, 1]`), which is why a post pass that samples with `uv()` looks right. A hand-made lookup `uv = p / span + 0.5` (v up) is mirrored | Look a y-up point up at `v = 0.5 - y / spanY`, and in a full-screen pass turn `uv()` into a y-up coordinate with `y = 1 - 2v`; keep ONE helper for both. Check with something asymmetric top to bottom. Found on Chromatic Impasto (2026-10-08): `ciCanvasUv` in `src/themes/chromatic-impasto/chromatic-impasto-tsl.js` |
| #114 | Need a surface that ACCUMULATES what is drawn on it (paint, trails, decals) on both backends, without compute and without a ping-pong | — | Draw into a `RenderTarget` that is never cleared: `renderer.autoClear = false`, `setRenderTarget(target)`, `renderer.render(stampScene, orthoCamera)`, then put the target and `autoClear` back. A `NodeMaterial` with `fragmentNode` and `CustomBlending` composites in place — premultiplied "over" is `(One, OneMinusSrcAlpha)` for colour and alpha alike, and the alpha a fragment returns is how much of the OLD value is lost, so a height channel can keep a share of what is under it. A whole-target multiply needs no read-back either: a quad that returns `vec4(0, 0, 0, k)` with `(Zero, One)` for colour and `(Zero, SrcAlpha)` for alpha scales the stored alpha by `k`. Half-float targets blend on WebGPU and on the WebGL2 backend. Nothing may sample a target in the pass that writes it: resample into a spare and swap, repointing the `texture()` node (`node.value = other.texture`). Precedent: `src/themes/chromatic-impasto/chromatic-impasto-canvas.js` |
| #240 | `Destroyed texture [...] used in a submit` (a WebGPU validation error) every frame, once a material reads `viewportSharedTexture()` and a `reflector()` mirror draws that material too | The shared framebuffer copy is ONE texture for every render target: the mirror pass (a smaller target) resizes it, which destroys the texture the main pass's bind group still holds in the same submit | Give such a material its own `viewportTexture()` (three keeps one copy per render target), create it ONCE and `.sample(uv)` it for every read (a node per read is a copy per read). Draw the reader after what it should see through (`renderOrder` among the opaques: the copy is taken just before the reader draws). Found on Crystal Cave's see-through crystals (2026-10-20), `backdrop` in `src/themes/crystal-cave/crystal-cave-gems.js` |
| #236 | A render-pass simulation (a fluid, a feedback buffer, a light carried frame to frame) comes up EMPTY in the game, though the playground shows it full; it fills in slowly over half a minute, if at all; no error | In the game three builds new pipelines in the background, and a draw made before its pipeline is ready is skipped (`renderer.info.render.drawCalls` does not move). The render pass's clear still lands (`autoClear`, with `resetRendererState`'s opaque black: alpha 1), and the ping-pong code swaps anyway, so every later pass reads a wiped target. A one-time seed pass (the resting state laid out at a seek) made while the theme builds is lost outright. The playground hides it: its capture mode re-seeks every frame | Never clear simulation targets (`renderer.autoClear = false` after `resetRendererState`; every full-screen pass covers its whole target), swap only when the draw happened (`drawCalls` before and after the call; its counters reset only between animation frames), and keep retrying a seed pass until it draws. | #106 | A radial push in a fluid solver built from render passes (a blast ring, a gravity well, an explosion) does nothing, or only flickers at its edge | An incompressible projection removes every force that is the gradient of something, and a radially symmetric push is exactly that: the pressure solve cancels it in the same step | Write it into the DIVERGENCE the projection must leave behind (a band of outward flow `u(r) = push · e^(−x²)` asks for `u′ + u/r`; a well is negative divergence, a spring positive) and let the pressure turn it into flow. Keep plain forces for what is not a gradient: a swirl, a directed jet, a ragged front. Precedent: the `asked` term in `src/themes/aether-tides/aether-tides-fluid.js` (2026-10-08) |
| #107 | A render-pass fluid solver runs for seconds, then flings its dye off screen and the picture empties, as soon as a well or a source is held on; no error | Net divergence has no solution in a closed box (clamp-to-edge pressure means no flow through the walls): the pressure climbs by a constant every step, and a half-float target soon cannot hold its gradient, which IS the velocity correction | Hold the pressure at zero on the domain's border so fluid may cross it (`p · step(1.5 texel, distance to the border)` in the Jacobi pass) and keep the visible frame inside a margin. `src/themes/aether-tides/aether-tides-fluid.js`.
