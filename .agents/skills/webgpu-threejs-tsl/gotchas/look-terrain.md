# Look: ground, heightfields, cliffs, trees and silhouettes

Read before building or changing terrain, dunes, cliffs, a procedural landscape, a hero tree, or a skyline painted in a sky function.

**The recurring lessons.** Size every noise read by the feature you want on screen (a baked tile packs several periods); keep displaced features at least three mesh rows wide and put finer relief in the normal; give long straight things something that varies along them; and size lines of light in pixels, not metres.

One gotcha per line; the number is the row's permanent id (cited elsewhere as "gotcha #NN"). Rows numbered below 300 were numbered before these files existed and keep their numbers, in no order. A NEW row goes at the END of this table and takes the next free number in **1600–1699**. To search: `node .claude/skills/webgpu-threejs-tsl/scripts/gotcha.mjs <words>` (`README.md` in this folder).

| # | Symptom | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|---|
| #183 | Near ground reads as crumpled paper, a field of spikes, although every displacement and mask read was meant to be metres wide | A baked multi-octave noise tile packs several lattice periods into ONE tile (High Coast's `bakeNoise`: 8, 16, 32 and 64 cells in r, g, b, a), so `texture(noise, xz / D)` has features D / period wide, 8–64× finer than D. A vertex displacement at that scale is finer than the mesh's rows, and a smoothstep mask of it has centimetre edges | Size every read by its channel's period (D = feature × period), blur the vertex read with an explicit `.level(2)` so the finest octave is gone, keep displaced features at least three mesh rows wide, and put finer relief in the normal only. High Coast's summit (2026-10-13), `swellAt` / `nearHollow` in `src/themes/high-coast/high-coast-land.js` |
| #184 | A heightfield cliff reads as melted wax or a draped sheet | A smooth height function has no horizontal structure: the face shades by its slope alone, and the field's posts (metres apart) round every edge | Cut the face into risers and ledges in the height function (`terrace(h, step, shift)`) and shade it in face space: the coordinate along the face (`dot(xz, normalize(perp(∇h)))`) and the height drive ledges, joints, a tone per block and ink streaks below the ledges. High Coast's headland (2026-10-13), `terrace` in `high-coast-plan.js`, `cliffFace` in `high-coast-land.js` |
| #181 | A ruler-straight plank or beam crosses a procedural landscape, the same colour along its whole length | Something is constant along a long straight line: a ridge whose crest is level carries ONE bed (geological height is constant along it), or a material mask switches at a fixed height above a floor that runs beside a straight valley | Give every long crest saddles and summits (crest height varying by tens of metres along it) and break every height threshold with low-frequency noise (`above + noise * 70`, wide `smoothstep`). Find the culprit by tracing rays through the beam's pixels on the CPU and printing the world points: they fall on a line. Painted Ages (2026-10-12): `SPINE` in `painted-ages-plan.js`, `plain` in `painted-ages-land.js` |
| #180 | A band of light in a geological height (strata glow, a contour sweep, a lit bed) floods a whole plain or gentle slope at once, while on steep faces it is a thin line | A band metres thick in the height coordinate covers as many metres of ground as the slope is gentle: on a flat it is a field | Size the band in PIXELS: half-width `fwidth(g) * 3` (a line a few pixels wide wherever the band meets the land) plus a faint term of the band's own width, and scale strata light by the slope (`smoothstep(0.06, 0.32, \|∇h\|)`) so alluvium and flats take little of it. Found on Painted Ages' clear waves and lock ribbons (2026-10-12), `ribbonLight` / `waveLight` in `src/themes/painted-ages/painted-ages-events.js` |
| #245 | Contour or scan lines drawn on a generated heightfield (cross-sections at fixed distances, level lines) read as jagged lightning, though the shading looks fine | The noise has octaves shorter than about two of the mesh's vertex steps: they cannot be represented, so they alias into per-vertex jitter, and a line that follows the surface traces every bit of it (shading hides it; a crisp line does not) | Cap the octaves per band at what its mesh holds (wavelength ≥ ~2–3 vertex steps along the coarser direction) and let the shader carry finer detail if any is wanted. Neon Dusk's ranges went from six to four ridged octaves and their scan lines became smooth curves (2026-10-21), `RANGES[].octaves` in `src/themes/neon-dusk/neon-dusk-mountains.js` |
| #250 | A lit crest line on a far heightfield (a dune brink, a ridge rim catching a low sun) breaks into a chain of beads, the same at every tier, though its width is antialiased | Far off, a geometric grid is slivers several pixels wide and under a pixel tall, and the lit band (the crest top under a grazing sun) is about one row high: it shows only where a triangle happens to expose it. Widening it (`fwidth`-based or not) changes nothing, because the hidden triangles are the problem | Bisect the shading terms (zero direct light, glow, specular in turn) to find what lights the beads, then filter the normal's turn over the crest onto the side that faces the eye as distance grows, so the lit band lands on triangles that are always visible. `lean` in `createSandMaterial`, `src/themes/shifting-sands/shifting-sands-terrain.js` (2026-10-21) |
| #251 | Wind ripples or any fine stripe pattern fold into closed rings and wildly varying spacing a hundred units from the origin, while near it they look right | The phase was `dot(worldXZ, dir(x)) / λ(x)` with a direction and a wavelength that vary with position: their derivatives multiply the whole world coordinate, so far out they dominate the phase's gradient | Keep the direction and wavelength fixed and bend and breathe the stripes with ADDED noise offsets of bounded size (`dot(xz, W) / λ + n·k`); the gradient then stays near `W / λ` everywhere. The ripples in `src/themes/shifting-sands/shifting-sands-terrain.js` (2026-10-21) |
| #123 | Sparkle or any per-cell point pattern on ground seen at a grazing angle is crisp underfoot and a shimmer of sub-pixel slivers further out, or swims when the camera drifts | A grid in world metres foreshortens with distance (a square cell at 30 m is sixty pixels wide and one tall), and a grid in screen space is not fixed to the ground | Lay the grid out in the coordinates the eye projects along, measured from the RESTING eye rather than the live camera: `(x / -z, eyeHeight / horizontalDistance) * density`. A cell is then a few pixels at any range, yet it is a function of the world point only, so it stays put while the camera drifts. Set `density` from the viewport on resize, not per frame. Precedent: `glintCell` in `src/themes/winter/winter-ground.js` |
| #187 | A hero tree's crown reads as broccoli (a pile of round lumps), or its alpha-tested needle or leaf cards thin out and vanish as the camera pulls back | Closed noise-pushed spheres have no sky between them and no fine edge. And a box-filtered mip chain averages a card's alpha DOWN: thin needles at 30 % coverage become a uniform 0.3, which an alpha test at 0.5 throws away entirely (without MSAA nothing else can save them; with it, three's `alphaTest` + `alphaToCoverage` sharpens the same threshold to a pixel) | Build the crown as plates of tufts on a branching skeleton, each tuft two crossed cards (`maskNode = tex.a > 0.5`, `DoubleSide`) carrying its plate's dome normal, its depth in the plate and its openness to the sky (traced on the CPU against the other plates), so a plate shades as a mass, dark underneath, gold on top, glowing at its thin edge when backlit. Draw the needle texture in code and build its mips on the CPU, rescaling each level's alpha (a binary search on a scale factor) until the share of texels above the threshold equals level 0's; give it as `DataTexture.mipmaps` with `generateMipmaps = false`. High Coast's old pine (2026-10-13), `bakeNeedleTexture` / `needleGeometry` in `src/themes/high-coast/high-coast-pine.js` |
| #140 | A far wood, ridge or skyline painted in a sky function reads as a dark curtain with teeth hanging DOWN from the top of the frame | The silhouettes were given the mist's colour (aerial perspective) while the sky above them fell to its dark zenith colour: the pale shape is the wood, and the eye takes the dark sky for the object | Make the low sky the mist itself and a little brighter than anything that stands in it (`haze * 1.3` up to the tallest silhouette, the zenith colour only above that), so every far shape is darker than what is behind it. Found on Stillwater (2026-10-09), `swBackdrop` in `src/themes/stillwater/stillwater-tsl.js` |
| #270 | A procedural treeline or silhouette drawn in a sky function puts a pixel-wide vertical line up the whole sky above every tree | The silhouette's half-width was `clamp(1 − rise, 0, 1) × width`: above the tip it is 0, not negative, and an anti-aliased edge `smoothstep(fuzz, −fuzz, \|x − cx\| − half)` still lights the pixels within `fuzz` of the centre all the way up | Let the half-width go negative above the tip (`(1 − rise) × width`, unclamped), and read the silhouette only below its tallest tip. `woods` in `src/themes/starlight/starlight-tsl.js` (2026-10-21) |
