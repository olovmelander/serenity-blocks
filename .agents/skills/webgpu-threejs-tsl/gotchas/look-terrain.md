# Look: ground, heightfields, cliffs, trees and silhouettes

Read before building or changing terrain, dunes, cliffs, a procedural landscape, a hero tree, or a skyline painted in a sky function.

**The recurring lessons.** Size every noise read by the feature you want on screen (a baked tile packs several periods); keep displaced features at least three mesh rows wide and put finer relief in the normal; give long straight things something that varies along them; and size lines of light in pixels, not metres.

One gotcha per line; the number is the row's permanent id (cited elsewhere as "gotcha #NN"). Rows numbered below 300 were numbered before these files existed and keep their numbers, in no order. A NEW row goes at the END of this table and takes the next free number in **1600–1699**. To search: `node .claude/skills/webgpu-threejs-tsl/scripts/gotcha.mjs <words>` (`README.md` in this folder).

| #183 | Near ground reads as crumpled paper, a field of spikes, although every displacement and mask read was meant to be metres wide | A baked multi-octave noise tile packs several lattice periods into ONE tile (High Coast's `bakeNoise`: 8, 16, 32 and 64 cells in r, g, b, a), so `texture(noise, xz / D)` has features D / period wide, 8–64× finer than D. A vertex displacement at that scale is finer than the mesh's rows, and a smoothstep mask of it has centimetre edges | Size every read by its channel's period (D = feature × period), blur the vertex read with an explicit `.level(2)` so the finest octave is gone, keep displaced features at least three mesh rows wide, and put finer relief in the normal only. High Coast's summit (2026-10-13), `swellAt` / `nearHollow` in `src/themes/high-coast/high-coast-land.js` |
| #184 | A heightfield cliff reads as melted wax or a draped sheet | A smooth height function has no horizontal structure: the face shades by its slope alone, and the field's posts (metres apart) round every edge | Cut the face into risers and ledges in the height function (`terrace(h, step, shift)`) and shade it in face space: the coordinate along the face (`dot(xz, normalize(perp(∇h)))`) and the height drive ledges, joints, a tone per block and ink streaks below the ledges. High Coast's headland (2026-10-13), `terrace` in `high-coast-plan.js`, `cliffFace` in `high-coast-land.js` |
| #181 | A ruler-straight plank or beam crosses a procedural landscape, the same colour along its whole length | Something is constant along a long straight line: a ridge whose crest is level carries ONE bed (geological height is constant along it), or a material mask switches at a fixed height above a floor that runs beside a straight valley | Give every long crest saddles and summits (crest height varying by tens of metres along it) and break every height threshold with low-frequency noise (`above + noise * 70`, wide `smoothstep`). Find the culprit by tracing rays through the beam's pixels on the CPU and printing the world points: they fall on a line. Painted Ages (2026-10-12): `SPINE` in `painted-ages-plan.js`, `plain` in `painted-ages-land.js` |
| #180 | A band of light in a geological height (strata glow, a contour sweep, a lit bed) floods a whole plain or gentle slope at once, while on steep faces it is a thin line | A band metres thick in the height coordinate covers as many metres of ground as the slope is gentle: on a flat it is a field | Size the band in PIXELS: half-width `fwidth(g) * 3` (a line a few pixels wide wherever the band meets the land) plus a faint term of the band's own width, and scale strata light by the slope (`smoothstep(0.06, 0.32, \|∇h\|)`) so alluvium and flats take little of it. Found on Painted Ages' clear waves and lock ribbons (2026-10-12), `ribbonLight` / `waveLight` in `src/themes/painted-ages/painted-ages-events.js` |
| #245 | Contour or scan lines drawn on a generated heightfield (cross-sections at fixed distances, level lines) read as jagged lightning, though the shading looks fine | The noise has octaves shorter than about two of the mesh's vertex steps: they cannot be represented, so they alias into per-vertex jitter, and a line that follows the surface traces every bit of it (shading hides it; a crisp line does not) | Cap the octaves per band at what its mesh holds (wavelength ≥ ~2–3 vertex steps along the coarser direction) and let the shader carry finer detail if any is wanted. | #250 | A lit crest line on a far heightfield (a dune brink, a ridge rim catching a low sun) breaks into a chain of beads, the same at every tier, though its width is antialiased | Far off, a geometric grid is slivers several pixels wide and under a pixel tall, and the lit band (the crest top under a grazing sun) is about one row high: it shows only where a triangle happens to expose it. Widening it (`fwidth`-based or not) changes nothing, because the hidden triangles are the problem | Bisect the shading terms (zero direct light, glow, specular in turn) to find what lights the beads, then filter the normal's turn over the crest onto the side that faces the eye as distance grows, so the lit band lands on triangles that are always visible. `lean` in `createSandMaterial`, `src/themes/shifting-sands/shifting-sands-terrain.js` (2026-10-21) |
| #251 | Wind ripples or any fine stripe pattern fold into closed rings and wildly varying spacing a hundred units from the origin, while near it they look right | The phase was `dot(worldXZ, dir(x)) / λ(x)` with a direction and a wavelength that vary with position: their derivatives multiply the whole world coordinate, so far out they dominate the phase's gradient | Keep the direction and wavelength fixed and bend and breathe the stripes with ADDED noise offsets of bounded size (`dot(xz, W) / λ + n·k`); the gradient then stays near `W / λ` everywhere. The ripples in `src/themes/shifting-sands/shifting-sands-terrain.js` (2026-10-21) |
| #123 | Sparkle or any per-cell point pattern on ground seen at a grazing angle is crisp underfoot and a shimmer of sub-pixel slivers further out, or swims when the camera drifts | A grid in world metres foreshortens with distance (a square cell at 30 m is sixty pixels wide and one tall), and a grid in screen space is not fixed to the ground | Lay the grid out in the coordinates the eye projects along, measured from the RESTING eye rather than the live camera: `(x / -z, eyeHeight / horizontalDistance) * density`. A cell is then a few pixels at any range, yet it is a function of the world point only, so it stays put while the camera drifts. Set `density` from the viewport on resize, not per frame. Precedent: `glintCell` in `src/themes/winter/winter-ground.js` |
| #187 | A hero tree's crown reads as broccoli (a pile of round lumps), or its alpha-tested needle or leaf cards thin out and vanish as the camera pulls back | Closed noise-pushed spheres have no sky between them and no fine edge. And a box-filtered mip chain averages a card's alpha DOWN: thin needles at 30 % coverage become a uniform 0.3, which an alpha test at 0.5 throws away entirely (without MSAA nothing else can save them; with it, three's `alphaTest` + `alphaToCoverage` sharpens the same threshold to a pixel) | Build the crown as plates of tufts on a branching skeleton, each tuft two crossed cards (`maskNode = tex.a > 0.5`, `DoubleSide`) carrying its plate's dome normal, its depth in the plate and its openness to the sky (traced on the CPU against the other plates), so a plate shades as a mass, dark underneath, gold on top, glowing at its thin edge when backlit. Draw the needle texture in code and build its mips on the CPU, rescaling each level's alpha (a binary search on a scale factor) until the share of texels above the threshold equals level 0's; give it as `DataTexture.mipmaps` with `generateMipmaps = false`. High Coast's old pine (2026-10-13), `bakeNeedleTexture` / `needleGeometry` in `src/themes/high-coast/high-coast-pine.js` |
| #140 | A far wood, ridge or skyline painted in a sky function reads as a dark curtain with teeth hanging DOWN from the top of the frame | The silhouettes were given the mist's colour (aerial perspective) while the sky above them fell to its dark zenith colour: the pale shape is the wood, and the eye takes the dark sky for the object | Make the low sky the mist itself and a little brighter than anything that stands in it (`haze * 1.3` up to the tallest silhouette, the zenith colour only above that), so every far shape is darker than what is behind it. Found on Stillwater (2026-10-09), `swBackdrop` in `src/themes/stillwater/stillwater-tsl.js` |
| #270 | A procedural treeline or silhouette drawn in a sky function puts a pixel-wide vertical line up the whole sky above every tree | The silhouette's half-width was `clamp(1 − rise, 0, 1) × width`: above the tip it is 0, not negative, and an anti-aliased edge `smoothstep(fuzz, −fuzz, \|x − cx\| − half)` still lights the pixels within `fuzz` of the centre all the way up | Let the half-width go negative above the tip (`(1 − rise) × width`, unclamped), and read the silhouette only below its tallest tip. `woods` in `src/themes/starlight/starlight-tsl.js` (2026-10-21) |
| #1600 | A far range of sharp summits painted in a sky function (behind real fells) reads as a striped ice wall, then as a row of pyramids | First try: the crest was a ridged noise read along the bearing, which gives flat-topped slabs with cliff sides, and the rock texture was a read stretched down the face, which draws vertical stripes: a glacier front. Second try: hashed tent summits alone (`tall − \|b − centre\| · slope`), which are ruled straight lines with both faces lit alike | Three things together. The skyline is the highest of two rows of hashed tents (great summits and lesser ones, three neighbour cells each, no texture read, so it is sharp at any size). The bearing is bent by one slow noise read before the tents are laid on it and the same read notches the crest, so no flank is a ruled line. And the faces are two-toned by which side of its summit a pixel is on (`clamp((b − centre) · k, −1, 1)`: moonlit one side, shaded and warmed by the twilight the other), with ribs read in the fan about the summit (`(b − centre) / (top − elevation)`), so the streaks run down each face from the top. Pale it toward the sky colour already computed for the pixel, soften the edge by about a pixel with a `smoothstep` on the crest, and fade anything drawn at its own depth behind it (shooting stars). `wRange` in `src/themes/winter/winter-tsl.js` (2026-11-16) |
| #1601 | A hero conifer's needles built as real geometry (serrated blades crossed about each shoot, the way a broadleaf spray's leaves are built) read as paddle leaves up close and as lobed blobs at the game's distance, however the teeth are cut: thinner and the crown is wisps, fuller and it is a hedge of leaves | A needle is finer than a pixel from twenty metres, so no triangle's outline can be one. A blade's teeth are a few pixels each and read as the lobes of a leaf, and the count that would make them bristles (dozens a shoot, thousands of shoots) is a million triangles | Keep the tree's real structure (bole, limbs, twigs, one spray per branch end carrying its sky visibility and bent normal) and make only the last level a drawing: each bottlebrush shoot is three slim ribbons, two vertices wide, crossed about its axis, their UVs laid for a drawn bottlebrush whose mip chain keeps its coverage, cut by alpha to coverage on a multisampled pass and by `maskNode` elsewhere (the twig that carries them stays solid: `mix(1, card.a, isNeedle)`). Slim ribbons (a brush about 1:3) and three years of whorls per spray give the grain; a wide ribbon reads as a blade again whatever is drawn on it, and a drawing dense enough to be solid at the first mip does too. Light the underside as well: a crown is seen from below against the sky, so add the ground's bounce and the sun that comes through the needles. `pine_cards` in `scripts/blender/high_coast/needles.py`, `needleMaterial` in `src/themes/high-coast/high-coast-old-pine.js` (2026-11-17) |
| #1602 | Need a forest that stands 500 m to several kilometres from the eye (its trees five to sixty pixels tall) to read as real trees with a lit side and a shaded one at every hour, not as blobs and cones | Instanced meshes at that size are either toys (an icosahedron a clump, a lathe cone) or millions of wasted vertices, and a sprite that carries baked colour is lit for one sun only | Bake each tree, and small groups of them for the far sites that stand for several, in front elevation into two sheets with one layout: shading data (shade, needles or wood, hue seed or bark mask, coverage) and the crown's normal in the sprite's frame, where a needle's normal is its crown's (its site's open direction blended with the hull's shape) and never its facet's. Draw one quad a tree, turned to the eye about the vertical, colour it from the data and light it through the normal (turned into the world by the quad's own right and toward-eye vectors; mirror its x with the tile). Clear gutters between tiles (12 px at a 256 px tile) keep a small mip off its neighbour, and a tile keeps its own proportions: a tree stretched to a site's width is a fat tree, so a far site gets a group tile instead. 22,000 trees are then 44,000 triangles and one draw. `bake_sprites` in `scripts/blender/high_coast/preview.py`, `spriteMaterial` and `siteSprite` in `src/themes/high-coast/high-coast-forest.js` (2026-11-17) |
| #1603 | The joints, blocks and ink streaks drawn on a heightfield's steep faces swirl like the grain of a plank or the bands of an agate, on every cliff and every island's rock band, at any distance | They were drawn in a coordinate "along the face" taken per pixel from the slope's own direction (`dot(xz, perpendicular(gradient))`). The direction turns a little from pixel to pixel, and with positions hundreds of metres from the origin a small turn moves the coordinate by metres: it is not one coordinate, and every line drawn in it bends with the terrain's curvature | Stand the joints in the rock, not in the face: two sets of upright planes, one across world x and one across world z (each wobbled by a broad noise read), and show on a face the set that crosses it by mixing the two RESULTS with the square of the face's level direction (`facing.x²`); ledges come from the height alone. Never mix the two coordinates themselves: a mixed coordinate swirls exactly as the old one did. The same for anything read "across" the face (the streaks: two noise reads, mixed). `cliffFace` in `src/themes/high-coast/high-coast-land.js` (2026-11-17) |
| #1604 | Crust lichens (or any patchwork: moss cushions, puddles, stains) drawn by colouring the cells of a Voronoi net, each cell filled to its edges with a dark line between cells, read as crazy paving or stained glass, and a saturated yellow-green species among them as neon tiles | A cell tiling has straight shared edges and no gaps, which is what paving is; and every patch at full opacity hides the rock that makes it a crust | Draw each patch as a disc round its cell's own point (`F1 < radius`, the radius a share of the cell that varies per cell and is lobed by a fine noise), so patches are round, of many sizes, and leave bare rock between them; let a third of the cells grow nothing; keep the patch thin (about half opacity, the rock's grain showing through) and put the dark only at its growing rim; warp the lookup by a metre-scale noise so no outline is a circle; keep the bright species rare and dull. | #1605 | A thin bright line runs across a far mountain's flank, level with the crest of a nearer range that stands in front of it; it is the nearer range's "light behind its crest" | The rim light of each crest line was `exp(−below / width)` for every pixel just inside the crest, whatever lies behind it. Where the nearer range stands against the sky it reads as backlight; where it stands in front of a darker, farther range (a volcano's flank) there is no light behind it to see | Weight the rim by the brightness of what is already composited behind it, before the crest's own body is mixed in: `rim · smoothstep(0.012, 0.09, luma(sky))`. | #1606 | Need a sculpted volcano (or any cone) inside a sky function that draws the far shore as crest lines: a flat silhouette reads as a brown paper cut-out | Each range is one crest line and a flat body colour; nothing on the cone's face says it is a cone | Give the cone's own pixels coordinates: `x = (az − cone.az) / halfWidth` (skewed per side), `hf = alt / peak`. | #1607 | A distant mountain range on a sky function reads as a neon zigzag in the twilight hours, or bristles with needle peaks | A lit band just under its crest (meant as snow taking the glow) traces the whole outline; the fold of a noise read gives a spike at every crossing, the low ones too | No crest band at all: make the range the PALEST silhouette (the most air in front of it: horizon glow × 0.4 + mid sky × 0.6 + the ridge colour), fading into the glow at its foot. | #1609 | A rock written as a distance field reads as drapery (smooth folds) or, once given beds and joints, as a brick wall | Lumps cut by planes in a space bent by a couple of sines have no detail between "one face" and "the whole lump", so their creases are soft folds. | #1611 | Need a clean line of light along a mountain range's skyline (a neon ridgeline, a rim of snow light): contour or scan lines drawn in the terrain's own shader never trace the crest, and an edge-on rim term lights every facet half turned from the eye | — | Take the skyline from the mesh on the CPU and draw it as its own ribbon.
