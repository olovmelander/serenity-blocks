# Performance: compile time, pipelines, frame cost and tiers

Read before any performance pass, after adding a heavy effect, when a theme freezes on entry or right after it reports ready, and when writing what a quality tier drops.

**Three separate budgets.** (1) Start-up: shader compile time and the NUMBER of pipelines (one per mesh per pass). (2) Frame time on the integrated GPU, which is arithmetic-bound: every term of a full-screen function is paid at every pixel. (3) Tiers: every tier keeps the whole picture and every event; a tier drops counts and detail terms, in JavaScript, never by feeding a neutral constant or a zero uniform. Measure with a verified instrument (ADR-0016); `docs/performance.md` has the method.

One gotcha per line; the number is the row's permanent id (cited elsewhere as "gotcha #NN"). Rows numbered below 300 were numbered before these files existed and keep their numbers, in no order. A NEW row goes at the END of this table and takes the next free number in **1100–1199**. To search: `node .claude/skills/webgpu-threejs-tsl/scripts/gotcha.mjs <words>` (`README.md` in this folder).

| #100 | A sky dome's shader (stars, aurora, nebula) costs the whole frame although ground, water and props cover most of it | The dome was drawn first (`renderOrder` low, `depthTest = false`), so every pixel ran its shader before being painted over, in the mirror's second render too | Give the solids the lower `renderOrder`s (near things first), draw the dome LAST among the opaque parts with `depthTest = true`, `depthWrite = false`: it is shaded only where the sky shows. | #269 | A full-screen sky function costs 20–45 % more on an integrated GPU after features that only matter in part of it (dust near a galaxy's plane, hills and mist on the horizon, a treeline), though a discrete GPU shows no difference | Every term is evaluated at every pixel, the water's mirrored pixels included; a 2-CU iGPU is ALU-bound on one big fragment | Wrap each regional feature in `If(region, () => …)` on the direction it reads (galactic latitude, altitude, density): sky regions are large and coherent, so whole waves skip the work. Measure old against new interleaved on the iGPU (`force_low_power_gpu`), with `nodeFrame.update()` before each `seek`. | #262 | A sky-shader change costs nothing at High on an integrated GPU but 25 % more at Low | At High the full-screen sky is held up by its texture reads (a 3D noise volume, several taps), and new arithmetic hides in their latency; Low reads less and is bound by arithmetic, so every added term shows | Time the lean tiers on the iGPU on their own (`force_low_power_gpu`, old and new served side by side, interleaved), and put the richer terms behind a tier flag rather than tuning them cheaper. Serenity Warp (2026-10-21): `richFar` and `heroStars` in `serenity-warp-quality.js` took Low from +3.6 ms to +1.5 ms on a Radeon 610M |
| #257 | Event effects written into a full-screen pass (strikes, lightning cells, latitude streaks in `uniformArray` loops) cost an integrated GPU 5–11 ms during a burst, though each term looks cheap and the RTX shows nothing | Every pixel of the body evaluates every live event, including a noise fetch per lightning cell per pixel: ten cells are ten fetches on every pixel of the giant | Gate each event's body by its own neighbourhood inside the loop (`If(d2 < s² · 64)`, `If(abs(dLat) < 0.12)`, `If(k < 6)`), put the fetch in an inner branch nearer still and on the upper tiers only, and gate a flourish's noise by whether it shows (`If(shown > 0.004)`). Measure on the iGPU at Low (`force_low_power_gpu`), interleaved against origin/main served from a second checkout. Stellar Drift (2026-10-21): four lines went from +8 ms to level with the old theme |
| #135 | On the lower tiers only, part of a procedural surface is flat and bright (a crust lit all over, cracks everywhere) | A tier drops a noise octave by substituting the constant `0.5` for it, and a later term takes a RIDGE of that octave: `1 - abs(2n - 1)` of 0.5 is exactly 1, the ridge's maximum, so the missing octave reads as "on a crack" at every pixel | Drop the TERM in JavaScript when the octave is absent (`detail >= 2 ? max(ridge(a), ridge(b)) : ridge(a)`), never feed a neutral constant through a non-linear shaping function, and capture the lowest tier before calling a surface done. Found on Void Ember's photosphere (2026-10-09), `hairlines` in `src/themes/void-ember/void-ember-star.js` |
| #1100 | A fragment stage climbs past its size gate (48 KiB of GLSL) after a round of new features, and trimming a feature only buys a few hundred bytes | Inline readers of a nested heightfield (the land, its cover, its shadow) each work out the same blend weights for the same point again: three readers at three points is nine copies of a `min(min(min()))` chain, and a second light source inlines the whole specular lobe twice | Share before you cut: pin one pair of weights per point and hand it to every reader (`fieldWeights(xz)` then `landAt(tex, xz, w)`, `fieldScalar(..., w)`, `shadowAt(..., w)`), make the weight and the lobe pure laid-out `Fn`s (written once a stage), and let a new term read a texture sample an old one already took.
