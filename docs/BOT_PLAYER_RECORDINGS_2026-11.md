# What players built before the clear

The most instructive recording starts with an open left wall. The player creates a shelf, loads it with a straight piece, and completes a different part of the board several placements later. This gives the Serenity simulator a concrete question to test. Can it demonstrate a reachable release and a useful second clear before giving the shelf any credit?

## Player diagrams and saved games

DAC and roncli's illustrated single-player FAQ gives an example with an explicit intended result. In §3.3, panels 1–4 show the set board, a square completing one support row, stacked straights falling into the shaft, and ten further lines clearing. The steps distinguish eleven lines from two clearing waves. In §3.4 the authors describe switching to survival as piece speed increases. The developers' player manual shows the preview and explains the timing of incoming multiplayer rows. These are observations about what a player can see in that game. Serenity supplies its own queue, movement and pressure timing. [Illustrated sequence](https://roncli.com/quadra/faq/3/single-player) · [Remz and Dada's player manual](https://quadra-game.github.io/old/)

In an October 2000 account, HELLFIRE player spindizzy describes practicing recursive line attacks and preferring vertical Z placements. That preference suggests another orientation to test for a useful supported group. Whether any given move works depends on the visible pieces and the actual release. [Player account](https://synt4x.org/tgm/hellfire/quadra/)

I decompressed each recording, checked the declared length, then read its embedded player names, scores, line totals, frags and maximum-clear entries. The four distribution demos omit the maximum-clear field. In the Showdown games a player could own the largest single clear and still finish well behind in frags. The records give a reason to measure the *timing* of a Serenity attack against an opponent as well as its size. They do not identify the row that triggered it.

## Viewing method and limits

I inspected the three public video streams as local playback copies. I sampled the entirety of zirre's clip at three-second intervals and the compilation at seven-second intervals, then extracted the relevant board regions at intervals down to a quarter second, with additional samples around the first stripe. I sampled fawk's direct 640 × 480 recording every four seconds and stepped through five builds at half-second intervals. Zirre's upload is 1280 × 720 video of a monitor. The competitive compilation is 320 × 240 and includes cuts, a filmed screen, and statistics intertitles. These timestamps identify the displayed event. They do not measure original input latency or a target number of pieces per second.
