## What the controller knows and does

The local manager supplies the own-board state, visible incoming row count, and, in a standard duel with equal handicaps, the opponent's visible headroom. Only the current piece and up to three previews enter the search. The seed supplies the deal and individual timing variation. The planner never draws the bag to predict a fourth preview, reads future garbage holes, or predicts an opponent's placement. When a blind effect obscures locked cells, it pauses board-based decisions until the view returns. A partial blind on a pristine board leaves the controller active.

1. **Find legal placements.** The first ply enumerates rotations, kicks, translations, soft drops and hard-drop endpoints from the live pose. At a central high spawn on a completely empty board, horizontal and rotation inputs already reach every landing. The solver skips redundant descent there. Comparison with the previous exhaustive search matched all 5,994 endpoints, including their input paths and order, across 189 standard piece and spawn combinations. Every actual clear runs the labelled-body resolver through its last wave. Future plies enumerate straight drops on an open skyline and use movement search near the roof. The finite beam and work allowance can miss future tucks. [Reachability and search](../src/core/ai/gravity-search.js)
2. **Build and extend.** Two nearly complete adjacent rows with a gap in common direct attention to a possible receiving well. They earn no guaranteed payout. Closed lower gaps are skipped by the hypothetical straight-drop fill so the evaluator can consider the support row above. An actual reachable trigger with the *next* preview can supply a continuation immediately, including its settled waves and reward. Later-preview probes inform ranking. Only an ordered path through visible pieces can become a committed plan. Body identity, visible sequence, threat and reward state are checked again before the next lock. There is no catalogue of prescribed shapes.
3. **Fire or recover.** A proved release competes on actual score in score play and outgoing rows in versus. A current attack that exceeds a vulnerable opponent's visible headroom can take priority. A burst enters after the current lock. If a candidate would overflow, the controller first selects among placements that survive the known rise, using the settled highest cell. A proof can fire before a new burst when it leaves sufficient room. Future commands are reconsidered on the raised board. The finisher can still be foiled if the opponent clears before receiving it.
4. **Execute.** Search advances in deterministic work units, including between fixed ticks. The input scheduler issues the selected real controls, verifies the target before hard drop, and replans following a rejected command or a changed queue. High local difficulties use shorter input intervals. The two-bot allowance gives the top difficulty more search before the same reaction deadline. Three- and four-bot rooms keep the shared planning limit. Odyssey's authored reaction and action caps and the frozen campaign measuring profiles keep their existing configuration.
