# Theme event surge (2026-10)

Every theme's gameplay reactions accumulate instead of popping and restarting, and every world builds a
crescendo of its own as play intensifies. Asked for on 2026-10-22: "during fast-paced gameplay — rapid locks,
cascades, combos — the effects feel like they abruptly pop and reset. Instead of restarting, I want the effects to
accumulate and build up naturally as the action intensifies", for every theme, each in its own visual language.

## What was wrong (the audit)

Every theme was audited for its event → visual pipeline (63 themes, one report each). The same faults recur:

- **Single-slot envelopes restarted by every event.** `flash.at = now; flash.strength = s`, `pulse = 1`,
  `start = now`. A single right after a quad cut the quad's glow from ~0.9 to ~0.3 in one frame; ten clears in a
  row looked like the first one ten times, each snapping back before the next.
- **Single-slot or two-slot bursts, rings and waves reused round-robin.** Cascade waves arrive 360 ms apart; with two
  wave slots the third wave deleted the first halfway across the world (Geode, Bioluminescence, Galaxy, Lunara,
  Neon District, Nimbus Veil, Cinder Drift…). Pools overwrote the newest or the oldest whatever its strength.
- **Clears that wipe what the locks built.** Tornado released every ribbon on every clear, Nebula Flow freed every
  ember, Lunara emptied every spire, Voltage Storm's towers dropped their charge: the harder you played, the emptier
  the world.
- **Hushes and blackouts before a quad** (Aether Tides, Cinder Drift, Stillwater, Moonlit Greenhouse, Nebula Flow,
  Chromatic Impasto, Voltage Storm, Lunara, Chiral Gold, Rainbow Terraces, Stellar Velocity…): back-to-back quads
  blacked the world out in the middle of the surge.
- **Anti-build.** Several themes closed exposure or cut bloom as combo power rose, undoing their own build-up.
- **Cooldowns that drop events** (Supernova's re-arm, Parhelion's Sun Cross, Waves' dolphins, Pyrestorm's flash gap).
- **No memory of intensity.** Nothing knew the player had been on fire for twenty seconds.
- **Cross-cutting:** physics sends PERFECT_CLEAR ~360 ms after the clear's last wave, and the director pattern most
  themes copied turned it into a new one-line clear (replaying the quad, restarting its hush, firing a second great
  wave). Cascade depth and back-to-back were ignored almost everywhere.

## The shared pieces

- **`src/themes/shared/swell.js` — `Swell`.** An attack/release envelope where every hit ADDS to what is still lit:
  a weak hit after a strong one can only lift the light; rapid hits ride up to a plateau under a soft ceiling; the
  stored energy is bounded (each new hit is trimmed so the curve's future peak stays within the bound; the history is
  never rescaled, so a reading never steps down when a hit arrives), so the glow lets go soon after a frenzy. Two
  numbers of state (exponentials forget their past), deterministic in time. Hits may be scheduled a little ahead (a
  quad's light after its hush); they join only once a reading has reached their time, so the future never lights
  early. `exportState()`/`importState()` carry it through a rebuild in place. The drop-in fix for any single-slot
  envelope.
- **`src/themes/shared/gameplay-surge.js` — `GameplaySurge`.** The canonical events read into how hot play runs, with
  the same weights for every theme:
  - `punch` (instant rise, ~0.5 s fall): momentary kicks, flashes and nudges, riding on a floor under rapid locks;
  - `level` (0..1, climbs at most 0.22 a second, falls over ~3.5 s): casual play ≈ 0.1–0.2, a quick steady hand
    ≈ 0.5–0.7, a long chain 0.9+. The climb is rate-limited so a burst of fast play builds across the whole chain
    instead of saturating in two clears (the tray's Fever used to plateau 2.5 s in);
  - `fever` (0..1): fills over ~4 s of hot play (level above 0.55), empties over ~2.5 s once it cools;
  - `stage`: calm / warm / hot / fever, with hysteresis so a reading on a threshold never flickers between looks;
  - `lock()`, `clear()`, `levelUp()`, plus `crown()` (a late perfect clear on its own), `spin()` (a T-spin that cleared
    nothing) and `wave()`/`tspin()`/`b2b()` for themes that hear a clear's parts one by one.
- **Serenity Mode's effects tray, Build-up group.** *Rush* (twenty hard drops at over three a second, clears between
  plain pieces: the pace) and *Fever* (a twelve-clear chain with a T-spin and a four-wave cascade, ending in a quad and
  a perfect clear): the two scripts that show whether a world accumulates or pops.

## Rules every theme follows

1. No pop: a new event never cuts light or motion that is still playing; slots are taken from the weakest/oldest;
   events are never silently dropped by a cooldown (their energy joins what is playing).
2. Calm play looks as it did. The crescendo is the world's own (its creatures, light, weather, water, sky, camera),
   in four acts, and the hands resting lets it breathe back.
3. A hush before a big clear is as deep as play is cold: a quad mid-fever inhales, it does not black out.
   The payoff (the quad and the late perfect clear) blazes in colour, carried locally in the effect's own elements
   (pillars, rings, the crystal, the flock), never by lifting the whole frame to white.
   The late PERFECT_CLEAR (settled ~360 ms after the clear's last wave) is a crown on the clear still playing, never a
   second clear; cascade waves build on each other; back-to-back has an answer of its own.
4. Heat goes into emission, saturation, motion and density, not bloom haze; the iris closes a little as heat adds
   light, so colours stay saturated and the darks stay dark.
5. Reduce flashing (ADR-0021) scales every frame-wide flash; Reduce motion scales the motion; no full-frame flicker
   faster than ~3 Hz.
6. Uniforms and CPU numbers only: no new passes, no per-frame allocation.
7. A tetromino drawn as an effect (a lock echo, stamp, footprint, silhouette or piece-shaped glow) is one solid
   shape in one colour, never four cells: no seams, gaps or per-cell borders inside it, one outline round the whole
   piece. Aether Tides' echo is the reference (`aether-tides-fx.js`: the union of the cell boxes, each stretched to
   its joined neighbours' centres, with a soft fillet in the inner corners).

## Verification

Each theme was captured in the real game before and after (Serenity Mode on that theme, the tray's Fever and Rush
played through the mode's own player, frames at fixed offsets, one capture at a time under a lock so parallel runs
could not slow each other's clocks), on the RTX 5080, with the console checked for WebGPU/TSL errors; every final
capture logged none. Each before/after strip and the peak frames (the quad and the perfect clear, full size) were
reviewed before the theme was committed; where the payoff still washed to white (Halcyon Apex, Ice Temple, Wolfhour's
meteor strike) the theme went back for a second pass. Every theme's own unit tests pass and gained tests for the
three properties this pass is about: a weak event right after a strong one never cuts the light, a long chain reads
hotter than its first clear, and the world breathes back to calm when the hands rest.

GPU timings were not taken: the user runs many sessions in parallel and both GPUs are noisy (see earlier passes).
Most themes add uniforms and CPU numbers only; the shader additions are small and listed per theme in its commit
(typically more event slots behind per-slot branches, a few terms per pixel, no new passes).
