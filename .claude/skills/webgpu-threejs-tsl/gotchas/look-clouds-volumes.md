# Look: clouds, fog, steam, marched volumes and traced fields

Read before building or changing a ray-marched cloud, nebula, fog bank, plume or steam, a heap of cloud cards, or a sphere-traced distance field.

**The recurring lessons.** A march has ten to twenty steps: anything that lives in a thin band of density draws the steps as contours, and anything read at every step with coordinates that ignore height smears into streaks. So read grain once per pixel, march analytic profiles, light with terms that vary smoothly along the ray, and space the steps by what the ray actually crosses.

One gotcha per line; the number is the row's permanent id (cited elsewhere as "gotcha #NN"). Rows numbered below 300 were numbered before these files existed and keep their numbers, in no order. A NEW row goes at the END of this table and takes the next free number in **1500–1599**. To search: `node .claude/skills/webgpu-threejs-tsl/scripts/gotcha.mjs <words>` (`README.md` in this folder).

| #115 | The clouds of a ray-marched slab (a galaxy disc, a fog bank) smear into streaks that all lean the same way, as if combed along the view | The noise is read at every march step with coordinates that do not depend on height, so each cloud is a column through the whole slab; a leaning ray crosses many columns and averages them along its ground track | Read the grain ONCE per pixel, where the ray meets the slab's midplane, and let the march walk only the analytic profiles (layer thicknesses, the arm phase, what is in front of the dust). The clouds keep their edges and the walk costs no texture fetches. Found on Galaxy's disc (2026-10-08), `grainAt` in `src/themes/galaxy/galaxy-disc.js` |
| #127 | A marched cloud or fog slab is covered in contour lines, like a map, or in a hatched pattern where the step jitter shows | Its light was put on a thin shell of density ("the lobes' thin edges glow": a `1 - smoothstep(lo, hi, density)` term). Each march step is a slice through the field, and a term that lives in a narrow band of density draws that slice's contour; ten to twenty steps cannot resolve a shell | Light the slab with terms that vary smoothly along the ray: by where the sample is (how low it hangs, how far in from the lit edge) and by a tap or two of density toward the light. For a backlit ceiling the relief then comes from lit far cloud showing behind dark near lobes. Spend the steps where the shape is: march only the part of the slab that is not solid. Found on Voltage Storm's cell (2026-10-09), `src/themes/voltage-storm/voltage-storm-sky.js` |
| #244 | A cloud slab seen from a low lens (a storm's underside over water or a plain) smears its lobes into horizontal brush strokes; or, rebuilt as a height field of hanging lobes, its silhouettes break into stair-step dashes | Near the horizon every ray crosses the slab at a grazing angle: a fixed step count puts tens to hundreds of metres of ground between samples, so the volume averages lobes along the ray, and a height-field march tunnels past lobe tips between steps. A search round the closest miss flips from row to row into stripes, and jittered steps leave a screen-door grain that nothing averages without temporal accumulation | Keep the soft slab and let the step count follow the ray's run over the ground (`steps = clamp(run / ~40 m, march, 2 × march)`, run = `(t1 − t0) · length(ray.xz)`): steep rays cost what they did, only the long low ones take more. Recover contrast in the lighting, not the geometry: a product of light factors each with a high floor (0.3–0.6) holds a lit side within about twice its shadow. Voltage Storm (2026-10-21), `steps` in `src/themes/voltage-storm/voltage-storm-sky.js`; the height-field attempt is recorded in its doc |
| #150 | A ray-marched TUBE of cloud you fly down (a canyon, a tunnel) is a moiré of hatching round the vanishing point, however the start of each walk is jittered | Steps spaced by distance from the camera are wasted in the empty channel near the lens and far too long for rays that run nearly along the axis: such a ray crosses the wall very slowly, so a 12 % step at twenty units is two units of cloud | Walk each ray from where it leaves the open channel to where it leaves the tube's OUTER radius (`t0 = rInner / sin θ`, `t1 = rOuter / sin θ`, steps in a constant ratio): every step then moves the same share further from the axis, whichever way the ray looks, and sideways rays stop early. Draw the noise out along the axis, and read a second 3D texture baked from the SAME lattices without the finest octaves wherever the step is longer than the fine detail (`mix` only in the band between). Found on Stellar Velocity (2026-10-11), `src/themes/stellar-velocity/stellar-velocity-nebula.js`, `bakeCanyonNoise` in `stellar-velocity-tsl.js` |
| #116 | Procedural gas reads as confetti or flakes of paint: flat shapes with hard edges | A narrow `smoothstep` on low-contrast fBm is a cut-out, however soft the noise | Let the light swell and fade on WIDE ramps (square one for a bright heart) and let extinction draw the edges: `exp(-tau)` of a dust term that varies strongly is a soft threshold of its own. Thin dark threads come from `1 - abs(2n - 1)` cubed. `src/themes/galaxy/galaxy-disc.js` |
| #253 | A marched nebula or cloud reads as slate grey however its colours are tuned, though each of its light terms drawn alone is a clean hue | Terms of COMPLEMENTARY hue were added in the same samples: a teal glow plus an orange emission, rim or thread light is grey, and the march averages them over the whole wall | Keep one colour family where the gas is lit, in shade and glowing by itself (glow, shade colour, emission zoned between the two), and the complement only on narrow features (rims of dark heads, the forward-scatter lobe round the light). Find it by drawing each term alone through a temporary build flag and capturing them side by side. Stellar Velocity's second pass (2026-10-21), `stellar-velocity-nebula.js` |
| #182 | Clouds on the horizon read as rows of white dashes, or as piles of balls | A flat cloud deck seen at a grazing angle is squashed into dashes; cumulus built from sphere impostors with a hard union shows every ball's terminator | Fade the flat deck out below ~10° and put the horizon's cumulus on camera-facing cards far beyond the land: a heap of billows (a flat-based row, a middle layer, towers) united by a log-sum-exp of their heights with the normal as the same weights' blend of each billow's own, lumped outline and bumped normal from two lattice-noise reads, lit with a wide wrap so the HEAP has a form, not each ball, and a silver lining where the sun stands behind. Keep the banks off the sun's bearings at the golden hours. `src/themes/painted-ages/painted-ages-cumulus.js` (2026-10-12) |
| #248 | A heap of billows on a camera-facing card (cumulus, smoke) has a dark fringe round its outline against the sky, worst on a backlit heap | The outline is frayed by noise past every billow's rim, where no billow contributes a normal; the blend falls back to the card's own normal, facing the camera, which is the shade side when the sun is behind | Past every rim use the nearest billow's outward direction as the normal (`vec3(outward, 0.15)`, tracked in the billow loop beside the signed distance), and let thin edges pass some sun from any side. Precedent: `src/themes/nimbus-veil/nimbus-veil-cumulus.js` (2026-10-21) |
| #267 | A heap of camera-facing puffs (a cumulus tower) shows dimples or a pile of balls: its front billows darker than the ones behind them, though each puff is lit by a sound wrap term | The heap's form normal was built from each puff's offsets in the cloud's OWN frame (across the view, along it) but used as world x and z. The camera looks down −z, so the face standing toward the eye (a negative depth offset) got a normal pointing AWAY from it, and any cloud off the axis had its sideways normal turned too | Build the form normal from the cloud's own axes in the shader: `deep = normalize(puff.xz)` (from the eye), `across = (−deep.y, deep.x)`, `out = across·ox + deep·oz`, then weigh each puff's own sphere enough that each billow has a lit top and a shaded underside. Check a front-lit heap close up: the front must be its brightest face. Found on Rainbow Terraces' tower (2026-10-21), `formN` in `src/themes/rainbow-terraces/rainbow-terraces-cumulus.js` |
| #266 | A cloud sea (or any heightfield of rounded heads: dunes, foam, cushions) reads as crumpled foil or creased fabric across the middle distance, worst under a light from behind the eye | Its heads were a union of hemispherical caps at several sizes. A cap's slope is vertical at its rim, and a smooth maximum that narrows to nothing there still leaves a crease at every rim, so four sizes of caps lay a net of creases that the light draws as hatching | Make each head a paraboloid that runs on below its rim and join the heads with a log-sum-exp, `top + k·ln Σ exp((dᵢ − top)/k)` over the nine neighbouring cells (skip heads more than `6k` under the top: the sum barely moves): one surface, soft saddles, no crease. Let the small heads ride on the large ones' shoulders (scale them by the parent's height) so the valleys stay broad, shade by height and hollowness as well as by slope, and pin it by the slope's jump between texels (the 99th percentile fell from 12.5 to 5.3). Found on Rainbow Terraces' atmosphere pass (2026-10-21), `bakeSeaField` in `src/themes/rainbow-terraces/rainbow-terraces-core.js` |
| #172 | Camera-facing volume-slice steam (vertical sheets) shows a soft but dead-straight vertical edge across whatever lies behind it, in the same place in every frame | A density modifier shaped like a vertical cylinder (an eye-thinning mask round the axis) or a bulk self-shadow tap on a plateau-dense envelope: seen side-on, a cylinder's side and the terminator of an axisymmetric body project as vertical lines (Thermal Bloom's Iris dome, 2026-10) | Use smooth falloffs with zero slope at their edge (a quartic lens `(1 − q²)²`), make the body a shell (dense skin, thin inside) instead of a plateau, and let the self-shadow follow the billows (multiply the far tap by the puffs) |
| #173 | A leaning steam plume is cut by a dead-straight edge partway up, on its upwind side | The lean grows with height (quadratic), but the sheet is a quad with vertices only at its foot and top: between them it is a straight parallelogram, and the curved envelope walks out of it (Thermal Bloom's far columns, 2026-10) | Cut each sheet into rows (8 is enough) so it bends with the lean, or keep the lean linear in height |
| #218 | A ray-marched column (any volume whose proxy mesh is wider than the opening it rises from: a crater, a chimney, a valley) is drawn over the rim in front of it, and hangs down a notch in that rim like a curtain | The march starts at the proxy's front face with no depth test per sample, so samples behind the rim (down in the crater) are composited as if in front of it. Fading the density below the source is not enough: rays climb as they cross the proxy | Fade the density out below the RIM's height (`smoothstep(rim − 130, rim + 70, P.y)`) so the volume rises out of the opening, or narrow the proxy to the opening. | #233 | Need a thick glowing volume (a corona, a haze sheet) inside a march whose steps are far longer than the volume is thick (a geodesic march striding a fraction of the distance to a hole) | — | Integrate it per chord, not per sample: each step is a straight segment, and a Gaussian sheet of scale height H integrates along a line in closed form, `len · (√π/2) · (erf(y₁/H) − erf(y₀/H)) / (y₁/H − y₀/H)` (Winitzki's erf: `sign(x)·√(1 − exp(−x²(4/π + 0.147x²)/(1 + 0.147x²)))`), with the midpoint value `len · exp(−(y_mid/H)²)` where the chord runs nearly level (`abs(rise) < 0.02`, guard the division with a `select`). Sampled per step it draws contour bands. Colour the column once per pixel from its light-weighted temperature. Precedent: `CORONA` in `src/themes/black-hole/black-hole-lens.js` (2026-10-20) |
| #225 | A ray-marched fractal (or any dense distance field) is washed over with its glow colour: no blacks anywhere, though every term looks small | A near-miss aura summed at every march step (`glow += exp(-k · h / t)`) assumes rays pass close to the surface only near silhouettes. Inside a dense fractal EVERY ray runs close to stone for most of its march, so the sum saturates the whole frame | Drop the per-step aura, or keep only the ray's closest approach (`min(h / t)`) and glow by that once. Light the air locally instead (analytic glows round the light sources, occluded by the hit distance). Found on Kaleidosonic (2026-10-13), `src/themes/kaleidosonic/kaleidosonic-temple.js` |
| #229 | A full-screen sphere-traced field (a fractal, a cave) spends ~50 steps a pixel, most of them in open air, and its occlusion reads the step count, so step relaxation or a shorter budget changes the picture | — | Cone-march first: a `QuadMesh` pass into a `RenderTarget` of one texel per 4 × 4 block marches the block's centre ray while `h ≥ t · pixelAngle · (block half-diagonal + hit threshold + 1)`, storing the last clear `t` and the steps taken; the full-resolution march starts there (`Loop({ start: steps taken, … })`) and keeps counting. Store `t` exactly (a 1/64 grid value plus its remainder, each exact in a half float): a start rounded back by 0.2 % added a step to 40 % of the pixels and biased the occlusion. 51 → 18 steps a pixel, frames equal to sub-pixel vein placement, no block seams in motion. Precedent: the scout in `src/themes/kaleidosonic/kaleidosonic-temple.js` (2026-10-19) |
| #108 | A sphere-traced scene shows a paler disc or wedge on its ground or sea exactly where a group's bounding sphere meets it; the mirror image inside the patch is wrong | The march runs only inside bounding volumes, and the field includes the ground. A ray that met the ground BEFORE it reached the volume starts its march underground: the field is negative there and reports a hit at once, at the wrong place. A march allowed to run on below the surface does the same at its far end | Clip the interval's far end just below the lowest trough, and treat a hit on the first step whose sample is under the surface as no hit, so the closed-form ground path shades it. A rising ray drops the ground term altogether. Found on Fluid Dreams (2026-10-08), `src/themes/fluid-dreams/fluid-dreams-liquid.js` |
| #109 | A smooth union of balls shows a seam in refraction or tint where two large ones meet, while small droplets beside them swell into jelly mounds | ONE smooth-minimum radius is right for neither scale, and shading attributes (lens centre, tint) switch across the union's narrow width | Nest exponential smooth minima: a group's own union handed on as one term costs one `pow`, `exp(−d_g/k) = (Σ exp(−dᵢ/k_g))^(k_g/k)` (clamp the inner exponent so the power cannot overflow). Take the normal as the softmax-weighted sum of each ball's own gradient (one pass, no finite differences), blend shading attributes with weights about a metre wide, and refract through the sphere that kisses the surface (centre = hit − normal · radius) instead of through a blended centre. `src/themes/fluid-dreams/fluid-dreams-liquid.js` |
| #170 | A straight seam runs down the middle of every shape lit from the gradient of a 2D signed distance (a pillar, a capsule, a blob) — a hard line from the head to the foot, the same on both backends | Inside a shape a distance field has a crease along its medial axis (the ridge equidistant from two sides): the gradient flips direction across it, and so does any normal built from it (`abs(p.x)` in a capsule's distance is that crease written out) | Let the shape's own slope fade out with depth inside (`exp(min(d, 0) * k)`), so it rounds the edges only, and take the interior's relief from a separate smooth field. Found on Nebula Flow's pillars (2026-10-11), `edgeW` in `src/themes/nebula-flow/nebula-flow-nebula.js` |
