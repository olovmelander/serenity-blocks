# Look: gameplay events, particles and bolts

Read before designing how a theme answers locks, clears, combos and level-ups, and before building particles, bursts, bolts or anything aimed "at the board".

**The recurring lessons.** What lands behind the gameplay card is never seen, so events leave the card's edge toward the open stage. Light adds up: budget the TOTAL additive light per tier, give overlapping pools one hue and a capped level, and let reactions accumulate through the shared `GameplaySurge` (`src/themes/shared/gameplay-surge.js`) instead of restarting an envelope per event. Frame-wide flashes go through `flashScale()` (ADR-0021).

One gotcha per line; the number is the row's permanent id (cited elsewhere as "gotcha #NN"). Rows numbered below 300 were numbered before these files existed and keep their numbers, in no order. A NEW row goes at the END of this table and takes the next free number in **1700–1799**. To search: `node .claude/skills/webgpu-threejs-tsl/scripts/gotcha.mjs <words>` (`README.md` in this folder).

| #144 | A lock's effect "under the piece" is never seen: its ripple, splash or mark always lies behind the gameplay card | The view ray through a piece on the board meets the scene straight behind the card, which is exactly the part of the picture the card covers (and the post's calm zone dims) | Do not aim through the piece. | #120 | An additive particle system looks right at one count and is a white slab (or a dim speckle) at another tier, in a formation, or on a phone | Exposure was set per particle, so total light scales with the count: five times the particles is five times the light, and additive blending has no ceiling. | #121 | A ring of light or shockwave sent through a thick particle volume reads as a general brightening, not a ring | The wave was a spherical shell in world space. | #264 | Dust, pollen or seeds drifting in the air come out as a snowfall: every speck at once, white on the bright haze | The motes were lit by the light of the air round them (the haze's colour, a forward-scatter glow), which reaches every mote alike, so all of them stand over the haze | Light a mote by the DIRECT light at its place only, read from the same occluder the surfaces use (the canopy gobo that draws the sunflecks, `rbFleck(u, P, { lod: 'fixed' })` in the vertex stage), plus a trace of the sky's: most motes are then lost in the shade and a few glint in the sun's patches, exactly where the flecks land on the bark, a light in the air without a shaft. | #220 | Need ballistic event particles (bombs, a fountain's spatter) to read as a burst from kilometres away | — | Under real gravity a 600 m arc seen from 15 km takes twenty seconds and crosses a pixel or two a second: fly them under a time-lapsed gravity (Pyrestorm: 300 m/s² for bombs, 52 for the fountain) and launch each from an apex height (`vy = √(2·g·apex)`, flight = rise + fall to the landing point), and draw a bomb's whole recent arc as a strip whose rows are its closed-form position at earlier times (`clamp(age − (1 − s)·trail, 0, flight)`), the ribbon's sides oriented by the chord so it never twists. | #126 | A lightning bolt, arc or streamer built in the vertex stage tangles into polygons and loops at one end, while bolts that stand upright in the distance look right | Its sideways wander was measured in METRES (a share of the bolt's length) along an axis crossed with a fixed direction. A bolt that runs INTO the picture (from near the lens to a far tower) is hundreds of metres long and a few degrees wide on screen: metres of wander at its near end are half the frame, and the fixed cross product degenerates when the axis lines up with it | Wander ACROSS the view and measure it as an angle: `aside = normalize(cross(axis, toPoint))` at each point, offset = noise · reach · (the angle the whole bolt spans at the lens) · (that point's distance from the lens). Give limbs their length and their forward direction the same way (the axis with its component along the view ray removed). | #214 | A lightning bolt (a jagged ribbon facing the eye, its side vector taken from each vertex's screen tangent) draws as scattered dashes, with no error | Where the jagged path turns back on screen the tangent flips, the ribbon's two edges swap, and the quads between face away: `FrontSide` culls every other piece. A path that also wanders in depth flips more often | `material.side = THREE.DoubleSide`, and orient each vertex's side vector with the stroke's overall screen direction (`dot(across, perp(B − A)) < 0 ? −across : across`) so the ribbon never twists. | #1700 | An event that eases an offset in (a level-up races the cloud deck 5 km in five seconds) makes the whole sky jump at the moment of the event, and the playground shows nothing wrong at rest | The offset was a closed form of the last event's time, `RUN * smooth(0, T, time - eventAt)`, with `eventAt = -1e6` at rest: before the first event it already stands at its full value, and each event drops it to zero and climbs again, so the deck teleports back by RUN. A unit test that compares a run with the event to one without reads a difference of nothing | An eased offset that events add to is a RUN, carried on from where it stands: keep `from` and `at`, read `from + RUN * smooth(0, T, time - at)`, start with `from = -RUN`, and on an event set `from` to the current reading before moving `at` (both in the session snapshot). | #1701 | Need a ring or wave of light that crosses a whole landscape (near bay to horizon) and reads as one gesture, and need it visible at night as well as by day | A ring that grows at a constant speed in metres crosses the near water in a blink and then crawls for seconds at the horizon; a light ADDED to surfaces reads as a scanner line and vanishes on dark rock or shows as a decal | Grow the radius by e-folds (`from * exp(rate * age)`, width a share of the radius): it then moves at about one pace on the screen at every range. | #1702 | Event particles (sparks, chaff, pollen, petals) that glint nicely under a dark sky are invisible in a bright scene: over a sunlit field or a bright sky nothing shows, even through a long lens | They are additive light a couple of pixels across. Added to a dark background a spark reads; added to a background already near the top of the tone curve it changes nothing. The same makes a dust column drawn additively read as a pale beam of light, not as dust | Draw them as THINGS: a lit colour with its own cover, blended as light plus cover (`blending: CustomBlending`, colour `OneFactor, OneMinusSrcAlphaFactor`, alpha `ZeroFactor, OneFactor` so the target's alpha stays, `premultipliedAlpha: true`, output `vec4(lit * cover, cover)`). Give a flake a size cap in PIXELS as well as a floor (`size.clamp(pixel * 1.2, pixel * 5.5)`): sized to show sixty metres out it crosses the frame as a blob when the wind carries it past the lens. `lightAndCover` and `FLAKE_MAX_PX` in `src/themes/grainfields/grainfields-chaff.js` (2026-11-14) |

| #1704 | A soft particle cloud (a dust burst, a puff of smoke) reads as a ball, or as a bubble with a rim | One large Gaussian sprite is a ball however it is lit. | #1705 | Need a curl, whirl or spiral of light in the sky (a twist's answer) that reads as a curtain wound up, not as a pinwheel or a small galaxy | A two-arm logarithmic spiral drawn flat in view space is a galaxy whatever its colour, and giving it height by reading the same spiral a few steps lower stacks ghost arms: the steps are far coarser than the ribbon is thick | Draw the RAYS, standing on the spiral's footprint.
