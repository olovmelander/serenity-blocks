# Verdant Hills — asset attribution

Every file in this folder is a project-owned original. All but the baked land map and
cloud field (see the last section) are the Blender pack, authored procedurally in
Blender 4.5.9 LTS on 2026-10-09 for this repository by
`scripts/blender/verdant_hills_assets.py` (helpers in `scripts/blender/verdant_hills/`,
built on the Fall grove's branching, bark, spray and GLB library in
`scripts/blender/fall_grove/`, the Summer meadow's modelling kit in
`scripts/blender/summer_meadow/kit.py` and the Golden Forest's boulder recipe in
`scripts/blender/golden_forest/props.py`). It contains no third-party geometry, texture,
scan or photograph, and nothing reconstructed or generated by an image or 3D model: every
vertex comes from the code in those folders, and the generator fetches nothing from the
network. Licence: the same as the project (MIT, project-local).

`asset-manifest.json` records the size and SHA-256 of each file of the pack, the layout of
the sprite sheet, each tree's height and anchors, and for every prop its size and anchor
points.

| File | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| `oak-hero.glb` | The hero: an old open-grown English oak (*Quercus robur*), 15.5 m tall and 22 m across, with one long low bough reaching toward +X. One bark mesh plus the foliage sites. |
| `oak-field-a/b/c.glb` | The trees of the middle distance, each a clear bole under one full round crown: `a` a 12 m round-crowned oak, `b` a squat 9.5 m one whose crown is blown toward +X, `c` a 14 m tree with a taller oval crown (a lime's habit). |
| `verdant-foliage.glb` | The shared sprays, two variants each: `oak_spray` (a stiff twig with rosettes of 14–17 lobed leaves, for the hero) and `oak_tuft` (one domed rosette of 9–11 plainer leaves, for the field trees). |
| `verdant-props.glb` | `windmill`, `windmill_sails`, `wall`, `wall_head`, `gate`, `bench`, `boulder_a/b/c`, `sheep_a`, `sheep_b`, `post`. |
| `verdant-impostors.png` | Front elevations of the three field trees and of the old oak, for the far hills. |

## How the trees are made

- **Growth.** Each specimen is grown from a seed (`verdant_hills/species.py`), mass by
  mass: an open-grown oak is read by its billowing masses of foliage and the dark clefts
  between them, so a recipe first places the masses (lumpy ellipsoids with flattened
  undersides), then grows the scaffold that feeds them, then fills each mass with angular
  shoots and hangs a spray on every tip. The old oak has a massive short bole (0.8 m in
  radius at breast height) that swells and breaks at about three metres into five heavy,
  sinuous limbs; each rises, arches over and reaches out, and forks into an upright leader
  and spreading branches whose every joint is an elbow (an oak's zig-zag habit). Its 22
  masses stand in rings round a dome that is open inside. One long low bough leaves the
  bole toward +X, levels out at about five metres and runs out to x = 10.3 m, bare for most
  of its length, with foliage at its end. A field tree is one clear bole and a single full
  mass; `c` keeps a leader right through its crown.
- **Bark mesh.** Tapered tubes with a flared, buttressed, burred base. Vertex colour
  carries what the runtime needs: R = how far the wind may carry the vertex, G = the
  limb's phase, B = ambient occlusion (a Cycles bake of the whole tree), A = moss and
  lichen: in patches on the weather side of the bole (the unit direction
  `weatherSide` = (−0.8, 0, 0.6) in the file's extras; low down, fading out by 4.4 m on the
  old oak and 2.2 m on a field tree), on the buttress roots and along the upper faces of
  the heavy limbs; 0 on thin wood. The scaffold (bole, roots, limbs and the bough) comes
  first in the index buffer (`barkCoreIndices`), so a low tier can stop before the forks,
  shoots and twigs.
- **Foliage sites.** A tree GLB stores no leaves. It stores a point cloud of sites —
  position, orientation, size, wind response, a sky-visibility term and a bent normal —
  and the runtime instances the shared sprays onto them. A site is the tip of the twig
  that carries its spray. The sites are stored shuffled, so drawing every other one (or
  only the first thousand) thins the whole crown evenly.
- **Anchors.** The old oak's extras and its manifest record carry `swing` (a point on the
  underside of the long bough, five metres out from the trunk, where a rope swing hangs),
  `swingLimbRadius` (the bough's radius there) and `boughTip`, in the tree's own metres.
- **Leaves are geometry, not alpha cards** (`verdant_hills/leaves.py`). An oak leaf is
  obovate with four rounded lobes a side and deep sinuses between them (the lowest lobe is
  the small one at the base); it is two strips hung on the midrib, so it folds along it,
  and its lobes ripple. They crowd at the end of each shoot: `oak_spray` carries a rosette
  at the end of its twig, one on each short side shoot and a few leaves along it. The
  tuft's leaves are two-lobed. Vertex colour: R = distance from the leaf base (flutter
  weight), G = per-leaf id, B = shade inside the spray, A = 1 on leaves / 0 on wood. Spray
  space: +Y along the twig, +Z the lit side, +X across.
- **Sprite sheet.** `verdant-impostors.png` is a Cycles orthographic render of each tree
  (seen from +Z, so +X is to the right) with an emission material that stores data instead
  of colour: R = shade, G = leaf mask, B = hue seed (on bark: 0.5 + half the moss mask),
  A = coverage. The tiles are the three field trees, then the old oak; the manifest gives
  each tile's pixel rectangle, its size in metres and where the trunk stands in it.

## How the props are made

Props are built as arrays, part by part (`verdant_hills/kit.py`, `mill.py`, `props.py`);
Blender only bakes their ambient occlusion. They are glTF Y-up metres with the base at
y = 0 and the front toward +Z.

- **One material shades them all**, so every vertex says what it is made of. `COLOR_0`:
  R = tone, G = material code / 16 (sixteen steps, not the Summer pack's eight: there are
  eleven materials), B = baked ambient occlusion, A = wear.

  | Code | Name | What it is | Tone (R) | Wear (A) |
  | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
  | 0 | `timber` | weathered grey timber | per-board brightness | silvered and checked by weather |
  | 1 | `limewash` | limewashed masonry | patchiness of the wash, drifting across the tower | grime streaks under the curb, the stage and the window sills, and moss at the foot |
  | 2 | `white` | white paint | per-part brightness | paint worn back to the wood |
  | 3 | `tar` | tarred dark boards | per-board brightness | tar weathered grey and dry |
  | 4 | `stone` | limestone and drystone | per-stone brightness | lichen (most on the tops of stones) and moss (at the foot) |
  | 5 | `glass` | window glass | per-pane brightness | unused (0) |
  | 6 | `door` | door paint | per-plank brightness | paint worn at the foot of the door |
  | 7 | `canvas` | sailcloth | per-cloth brightness | stains, most toward the edges of a cloth |
  | 8 | `wool` | fleece | brightness of the fleece: its lumps are lighter than its hollows | soiled fleece: the belly and the breech |
  | 9 | `skin` | a sheep's dark face, ears and legs | per-part brightness | unused (0) |
  | 10 | `iron` | iron | per-part brightness | rust |

- **`TEXCOORD_0`** lays each surface out flat in metres, stored as 0.5 + metres / 32:
  v runs along the grain of a board (up a wall, along a rail, up the cap from its eaves)
  and u across it; the tower's wash is unrolled round the tower at its own radius. Glass
  panes are mapped 0..1 across themselves.
- **The mill** (`windmill`, no sails) is an English tower mill: a round tower of
  limewashed masonry 11.5 m to the curb, 6.2 m across at the ground and 3.9 m at the top,
  one smooth sheet with its openings cut out of it, on a low plinth of dressed stone that
  goes 0.3 m into the ground. A ledged plank door on iron strap hinges stands at ground
  level facing +Z, over a stone step and under a stone lintel; six small four-pane windows
  with white frames and stone sills climb the tower, each set 0.3 m into the wall. A timber
  stage (the reefing gallery) runs round it at 3.6 m: 32 deck boards on twelve bearers and
  raking braces, with twelve posts and two rails. The cap is a boat-shaped ogee in tarred
  boards (4.4 m across, 5.4 m fore and aft, 2.6 m high) with a petticoat over the curb and
  a white ball finial; the windshaft's iron nose leaves it on the +Z side.
- **The sails** (`windmill_sails`, its own node: the runtime spins it) are four common
  sails about the origin, in the XY plane, turning about +Z. Each is a tapering whip 9.2 m
  long with a lattice 1.9 m wide: a leading board ahead of it and, behind it, eighteen
  sail bars tied by two laths, all thin boxes. The bars are set to the weather (18° at the
  heel, 5° at the tip), which gives each sail its twist; canvas is spread over the outer
  two thirds on the +Z face, with a second face behind it. An iron boss sits at the centre.
- **The wall** is four metres of double-faced, battered drystone that tiles end to end
  along X: seven courses a side, thinner toward the top, every stone its own slightly
  irregular block standing out of its joints, with leaning upright joints; a stone that
  crosses the end of the section is cut there and its other half comes back in at the far
  end (the ambient occlusion is baked between two copies of the section, so the ends are
  shaded as the middle of a run). A row of cope stones stands on edge on top. **The wall
  head** is a stout pier of big stones that ends a run: it is set 0.12 m over the end of
  the last section (its origin on that section's end plane, +X pointing away from the
  run) and is a hand thicker than the wall and a little taller, so the cut stones end inside it.
- **The gate** is a five-bar field gate, closed, hinged on the −X post: a heavy hanging
  stile, a lighter shutting stile, five bars closer together low down, a brace and two
  uprights, iron strap hinges and a latch, between two dressed stone posts 0.45 m square.
  **The bench** is 1.8 m of plank seat and plank back on two end frames, facing +Z.
  **The outcrops** follow the Golden Forest's boulder recipe (a sphere pushed about by
  noise), then gathered onto level beds and planed off on top, as limestone weathers.
  **The sheep** are a lumpy fleece on four dark legs with a dark head and ears, facing +Z:
  `sheep_a` stands with its head up, `sheep_b` grazes. **The post** is one riven fence
  post, 1.3 m out of the ground.
- **Anchors.** Each prop's node `extras` (and its record in the manifest) carry its size,
  its bounds, its materials and named points in its own metres:

  | Prop | Anchors |
  | --- | --- |
  | `windmill` | `hub` (where the windshaft ends in front of the cap: set the sails' origin here), `axis` (the unit vector the sails face: +Z tilted up 8°), `door` and `doorSize`, `capTop`, `curb`, `baseRadius`, `topRadius`, `sailRadius`, `stage` (`height`, `innerRadius`, `outerRadius`, `railHeight`), `windows` (each `centre`, `width`, `height`, `normal`) |
  | `windmill_sails` | `radius` (9.2), `axis` (+Z), `sails` (4), `width` (1.9), `clothFrom` (where the canvas starts), `centre` |
  | `wall` | `section` (4), `start`, `end`, `height`, `bodyHeight`, `footThickness`, `topThickness`, `footing` |
  | `wall_head` | `join` (the origin: the end plane of the run), `end`, `overlap`, `length`, `height` |
  | `gate` | `hinge` and `hingeAxis`, `latch`, `postLeft` and `postRight` (the tops of the posts), `width`, `height`, `opening`, `postSize`, `bars` (the heights of the five bars) |
  | `bench` | `seat`, `seatHeight`, `length`, `backTop` |
  | `boulder_a/b/c` | `top` |
  | `sheep_a`, `sheep_b` | `head`, `muzzle`, `back`, `grazing` |
  | `post` | `top`, `tie` (where a line is made fast), `thickness`, `height` |

## Format

GLBs are written by the generator itself (`fall_grove/glb.py`), not by Blender's exporter:
positions are normalised 16-bit integers restored by the node's scale and translation,
normals and colours are 8-bit (`KHR_mesh_quantization`). There are no materials, textures
or animations in the files; the theme owns every material.

## Regenerating

```bash
blender --background --factory-startup --python scripts/blender/verdant_hills_assets.py -- \
    --out src/themes/verdant-hills/assets [--only trees,foliage,impostors,props] [--preview <dir>]
```

`--factory-startup` keeps the run independent of any Blender session that is open.
Growth and props are seeded and the bakes run on the CPU: on the machine that wrote this
pack, three runs in a row produced all seven files (and the manifest) byte for byte, and
so did a run with `--preview`. Re-run the tests after regenerating
(`tests/unit/verdant-hills-assets.test.js` pins every file's hash to the manifest). The
whole pack takes about half a minute on a laptop CPU; `--preview` adds several minutes of
stills. The generator writes its seven files and `asset-manifest.json` and touches nothing
else in the folder.

## Baked land map and cloud field

Four files are not part of the Blender pack and are not listed in `asset-manifest.json`:
`verdant-land.png`, `verdant-clouds.png` and the record of each, `verdant-land.json` and
`verdant-clouds.json`. All four are written by `scripts/verdant-hills/bake-land.mjs`.

- **`verdant-land.png`** is the valley's patchwork map (hedgerows, the tone of each
  pasture, still water and the shade of the far trees), computed from the theme's own
  terrain function (`createVerdantHillsLandData()` in `verdant-hills-terrain.js`) at a size
  the theme could not afford to work out when it starts. `verdant-land.json` pins its
  size, bounds and SHA-256.
- **`verdant-clouds.png`** is the sky's cloud field, 256 px square, computed from the
  theme's own light rig (`createVerdantHillsCloudData()` in `verdant-hills-light.js`). It
  stores data, not colour: R = how high the cumulus heaps, G = the billows that carve its
  flanks, B = finer billows; alpha is opaque. `verdant-clouds.json` pins its size, seed and
  SHA-256 and carries the table of its levels (`edges`: the height below which each
  256th of the sky lies).

Like the pack they are project-original and procedural: no third-party data, photograph
or scan goes into them and the script fetches nothing from the network. Without the files
the theme makes a coarse land map and the same clouds on the spot. Regenerate them with
`node scripts/verdant-hills/bake-land.mjs`.
