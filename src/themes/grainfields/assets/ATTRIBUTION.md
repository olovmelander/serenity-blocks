# Grainfields — asset attribution

Every file in this folder is a project-owned original, authored procedurally in
Blender 4.5.9 LTS on 2026-10-18 for this repository by
`scripts/blender/grainfields_assets.py` (recipes and sprays in `scripts/blender/grainfields/`,
built on the Fall grove's branching library in `scripts/blender/fall_grove/`, the Golden
Forest's conifer and needle builders in `scripts/blender/golden_forest/` and the Summer
meadow's birch wood and leaf outline in `scripts/blender/summer_meadow/`; the horse in
`scripts/blender/grainfields/horse.py`).
It contains no third-party geometry, texture, scan or photograph, and nothing
reconstructed or generated by an image or 3D model: every vertex comes from the code in
those folders, and the generator fetches nothing from the network. Licence: the same as
the project (MIT, project-local).

`asset-manifest.json` records each file's size and SHA-256 and the layout of the sprite
sheet.

Every tree is a grove tree: the fields show them from ninety metres to three kilometres,
never close, so there are no framing trees and no props. The horses graze about sixty
metres out, in barley that hides them below the knee.

## How the trees are made

- **Growth.** Each specimen is grown from a seed (`grainfields/species.py`). A birch is
  one tall white stem (or three from a stool) whose limbs climb at thirty to fifty degrees
  and barely sag, with the crown high on the stem: less weeping than a silver birch. The
  Ezo spruce is the Summer meadow's spruce (after the Golden Forest's) drawn in to a narrow
  spire, with six limbs or so to a whorl instead of five, the whorls a little closer and
  the bough sections a little larger, so the spire closes up. The larch keeps one straight
  leader to the top; its limbs leave the stem in loose tiers, nearly level low down and
  rising under the spire, and flat sprays of branchlets leave them sideways in the limb's
  own plane.
- **Bark mesh.** Tapered tubes with a flared base. Vertex colour carries what the runtime
  needs: R = how far the wind may carry the vertex, G = the limb's phase, B = ambient
  occlusion (a Cycles bake of the whole tree), A = a species mask. On a birch the mask is
  *dark bark*, as in the Summer meadow: 1 on thin limbs and twigs, on the roots, on the
  rough black foot of the stem and in a chevron under each limb that leaves a white stem;
  0 on the smooth white stem. On a spruce it is lichen. On a larch it runs from 0 on the
  thick, plated grey-brown bark of the lower bole to 1 on the thin, flaking red-brown bark
  of the upper stem and the limbs. The stems come first in the index buffer
  (`barkCoreIndices`), so a low tier can stop before the limbs and twigs.
- **Foliage sites.** A tree GLB stores no leaves. It stores a point cloud of sites —
  position, orientation, size, wind response, a sky-visibility term and a bent normal —
  and the runtime instances the shared sprays onto them. Each tree keeps between 160 and
  270 sites, the counts of the Summer meadow's grove trees, because the fields draw a
  couple of hundred of them.
- **Leaves and needles are geometry, not alpha cards** (`grainfields/leaves.py`). The
  birch leaf is the Summer meadow's kite-shaped far leaf; the spruce sections are the
  Golden Forest's; a larch tuft is a burst of the Golden Forest's pine-needle bundles from
  one spur shoot, opening up and out like a shaving brush. Vertex colour: R = distance from
  the leaf base or the spur (flutter weight), G = per-leaf or per-tuft id, B = shade inside
  the spray, A = 1 on leaves and needles / 0 on wood.
- **Sprite sheet.** `grainfields-impostors.png` is a Cycles orthographic render of each
  tree with an emission material that stores data instead of colour: R = shade, G = leaf
  mask, B = hue seed (on bark: 0.5 + half the species mask), A = coverage. It is the Fall
  grove's bake with 256-pixel tiles (the Summer meadow's are 448): at six hundred metres a
  tree is a few dozen pixels tall.

## How the horse is made

- **Shape.** A signed distance field (`grainfields/horse.py`) of ellipsoids and round cones
  (tapered capsules, some squashed to an elliptic section) joined by smooth unions: the
  barrel, belly, girth, chest, shoulders, withers, back, quarters, croup, stifles and
  thighs; the neck as a deep tapering core at about 47 degrees under an arched crest; the
  head as a cranium, cheeks, jaw bars, a tapering face, lips and chin, 48 degrees below the
  horizontal; leaf-shaped ears; four legs of forearm or gaskin, knee or hock, cannon,
  fetlock with a little feather, pastern and hoof, the hooves cut flat on the ground; the
  mane on the crest and falling to both sides, the forelock, and the tail (a dock lying down
  the buttocks, the hair falling from it and parting into locks), the hair lumpy.
- **Mesh.** The left half is sampled on a 1 cm grid, polygonised with surface nets and
  projected onto the field; its flat cut face is dropped, Blender's quadric edge collapse
  decimates it to 2,500 triangles, folds are collapsed and relaxed, and it is mirrored and
  welded on the midline, so the horse is exactly symmetric and watertight. Normals are the
  field's gradient.
- **Vertex colour** (`COLOR_0`, normalised unsigned bytes): R = part id / 8 (0 body, 1 neck
  with the head, ears, mane and forelock, 2 front-left leg, 3 front-right, 4 hind-left,
  5 hind-right, 6 tail; the horse faces +z, so its left is +x), each vertex taking the part
  of the element nearest it, the neck leaving the body through a plane from the front of the
  withers to the foot of the jugular groove and each leg below its elbow or stifle; G = a
  dark mask with a soft edge (mane, forelock, tail, the legs below the knees and hocks, the
  hooves); B = a light mask (a mealy muzzle and the belly); A = 1.
- **Pivots** (metres, the horse's frame, in the file's extras and the manifest): `neck` at
  the middle of the plane the neck leaves the body by, `frontLeft`/`frontRight` at the
  shoulder joints above the forearms, `hindLeft`/`hindRight` at the hip joints, `tail` at
  the root of the dock.

## Format

GLBs are written by the generator itself (`fall_grove/glb.py`), not by Blender's exporter:
positions are normalised 16-bit integers restored by the node's scale and translation,
normals and colours are 8-bit (`KHR_mesh_quantization`). There are no materials, textures
or animations in the files; the theme owns every material.

## Regenerating

```bash
blender --background --factory-startup --python scripts/blender/grainfields_assets.py -- \
    --out src/themes/grainfields/assets [--only trees,foliage,impostors,horse] [--preview <dir>]
```

The horse alone:

```bash
blender --background --factory-startup --python scripts/blender/grainfields_assets.py -- \
    --out src/themes/grainfields/assets --only horse [--preview <dir>]
```

`--factory-startup` keeps the run independent of any Blender session that is open.
Growth is seeded and the bakes run on the CPU: on the machine that wrote this pack, two
runs in a row produced all eleven tree, spray and sprite files (and the manifest) byte for
byte, and the horse likewise. The trees take about fifteen seconds on a laptop CPU and the
horse under ten; `--preview` adds half a minute of stills for each (for the horse: side,
three-quarter, against the light, parts, masks, head, and the herd in barley at sixty
metres). A partial `--only` run keeps the other files' manifest entries.
Re-run `tests/unit/grainfields-assets.test.js` after regenerating.
