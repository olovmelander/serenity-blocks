# High Coast — asset attribution

Every file in this folder is a project-owned original, generated for this repository by code in
this repository. It contains no third-party geometry, texture, scan or photograph, and nothing
reconstructed or generated by an image or 3D model: every texel and every vertex comes from the
scripts named below, and neither generator fetches anything from the network. Licence: the same
as the project (proprietary, all rights reserved).

## The baked coast

`coast-near.png`, `coast-mid.png`, `coast-far.png` and `coast-manifest.json` are written by
`scripts/high-coast/bake-coast.mjs` from the theme's own plan of the coast
(`src/themes/high-coast/high-coast-plan.js`): sixteen-bit heights, sky visibility and forest cover.
The manifest records each field's size and SHA-256.

## The trees

`pine-old.glb`, `high-coast-foliage.glb`, `high-coast-trees.png`, `high-coast-trees-normal.png`
and `trees-manifest.json` were authored procedurally in Blender 5.2.2 LTS on 2026-11-17 by
`scripts/blender/high_coast_assets.py` (recipes, sprays and the sprite bake in
`scripts/blender/high_coast/`, built on the Fall grove's branching library in
`scripts/blender/fall_grove/` and the Golden Forest's spruce recipe and needle strips in
`scripts/blender/golden_forest/`). `trees-manifest.json` records each file's size and SHA-256 and
the layout of the sprite sheets.

| File | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| `pine-old.glb` | The old Scots pine (*Pinus sylvestris*) on the summit knoll, 14 m to its leader and 17 m to the top of its crown: a stout bole that leans out toward the view and kinks where it once lost its leader, roots that grip the bare rock, fourteen living limbs that leave the bole nearly level and turn up at their ends, a bare silver limb and five stubs below them. One bark mesh, plus the foliage sites. |
| `high-coast-foliage.glb` | The old pine's two needle sprays (`pine_shoots_0`, `pine_shoots_1`): three years of a twig's growth, each year's node a whorl of bottlebrush shoots that lean out and turn up. A shoot is three slim ribbons crossed about its axis, their UVs laid for the theme's drawn bottlebrush (the needles are lines of that drawing, which the theme bakes at start-up; it is not a file). |
| `high-coast-trees.png` | The forest's sprites, shading data: front elevations of four Scots pines (13.5–20.5 m), three Norway spruces (*Picea abies*, 16.5–24 m) and three small groups of them. R = shade (sky visibility and the spray's own shading), G = 1 on needles / 0 on wood, B = the spray's hue seed (needles) or the bark's mask (wood: 0 the grey lower bole … 1 the orange bark above, on a pine; lichen on a spruce), A = coverage. |
| `high-coast-trees-normal.png` | The same tiles as normals in the sprite's frame (x right, y up, z toward the eye), A = coverage. A needle's normal is its crown's (the direction its site is open to, blended with the crown's own shape), not its facet's: the theme lights each crown as a mass. |

The forest stands half a kilometre and more from the eye, so its trees ship only as sprites; the
meshes they were rendered from are not in the pack.

## How the old pine is made

- **Growth.** The bole follows a hand-set centre line (`high_coast/species.py`, `OLD_TRUNK`); each
  limb leaves it at a set height, bearing and tilt (`OLD_LIMBS`), holds its course outward and
  turns up toward its end, and is branched twice. Spray sites stand along the twigs, thinned so
  none stands inside another. It is authored in the frame the theme stands it in: +X is the
  view's right, +Z is toward the eye, the rock is y = 0.
- **Bark mesh.** Tapered tubes, the bole's foot flared into its roots. Vertex colour: R = how far
  the wind may carry the vertex, G = the limb's phase, B = ambient occlusion (a Cycles bake of
  the whole tree), A = 0 on the plated grey-brown bark of the lower bole rising to 1 on the thin
  orange bark above. `_WOOD`: x = 1 on dead silver wood, y = how far a vertex follows the rock
  (the theme lays the roots on the summit's own relief). The bole and roots come first in the
  index buffer, then the limbs, their branches and last the twigs (`barkLevelIndices`), so a low
  tier can stop before the twigs.
- **Foliage sites.** The tree stores no needles. It stores a point cloud of sites — position,
  orientation, size, wind response, a sky-visibility term and a bent normal — and the theme
  instances the two sprays onto them.
- **The sprites.** Each forest tree and each group is rendered twice in front elevation by Cycles
  with an emission-only material (numbers, not light): once with the shading data in its vertex
  colours, once with the crown normals. Tiles are fitted to their crowns and laid in one row with
  a clear gutter between them.

## Rebuilding

```
blender --background --factory-startup --python scripts/blender/high_coast_assets.py -- \
    --out src/themes/high-coast/assets [--only trees,foliage,sprites] [--preview <dir>]
node scripts/high-coast/bake-coast.mjs
```

The tree build is deterministic for one Blender version (the sprite renders are path traced with
a fixed seed); it takes about half a minute. `--preview` writes stills that are never shipped.
