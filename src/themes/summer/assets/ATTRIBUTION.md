# Summer — asset attribution

Every file in this folder is a project-owned original, authored procedurally in
Blender 4.5.9 LTS on 2026-10-08 for this repository by
`scripts/blender/summer_meadow_assets.py` (helpers in `scripts/blender/summer_meadow/`,
built on the Fall grove's branching library in `scripts/blender/fall_grove/` and the
Golden Forest's conifer, needle and boat builders in `scripts/blender/golden_forest/`).
It contains no third-party geometry, texture, scan or photograph, and nothing
reconstructed or generated by an image or 3D model: every vertex comes from the code in
those folders, and the generator fetches nothing from the network. Licence: the same as
the project (MIT, project-local).

`asset-manifest.json` records each file's size and SHA-256, the layout of the sprite
sheet, and for every prop its size and anchor points.

| File | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| `birch-hero.glb`, `spruce-hero.glb` | The two framing trees: an old weeping silver birch (16.5 m) that leans toward −X and hangs curtains of shoots, and an old Norway spruce (21 m) with drooping boughs. One bark mesh each, plus the foliage sites. |
| `birch-grove-a/b/c.glb`, `spruce-grove-a/b/c/d.glb` | The trees of the middle distance: birches of 11.5–16.5 m (`c` has a forked double stem) and spruces of 12.5–20 m. |
| `summer-foliage.glb` | The shared sprays, two variants each: `birch_spray` (a hanging twig of 15–18 serrated leaves, for the hero), `birch_strand` (a drooping twig of 13 plain leaves, for the grove), `spruce_bough` (the finely modelled bough section, for the hero) and `spruce_frond` (the light one, for the grove). |
| `summer-props.glb` | `cottage`, `shed`, `maypole`, `jetty`, `rowboat`, `boulder_a/b/c`, `fence`. |
| `summer-impostors.png` | Front elevations of the seven grove trees for the far shore. |

## How the trees are made

- **Growth.** Each specimen is grown from a seed (`summer_meadow/species.py`). The old
  birch has a leaning, S-curved bole that forks into three ascending leaders; their limbs
  climb, arch over and let go of pendulous shoots up to six metres long that stop well
  clear of the ground, and one long bough sweeps out on the leaning side. A grove birch is
  one slender white leader (or two from one stool) in an airy oval crown. The spruce is
  the Golden Forest's recipe with its sprays and whorl spacing scaled to the tree's height.
- **Bark mesh.** Tapered tubes with a flared base. Vertex colour carries what the runtime
  needs: R = how far the wind may carry the vertex, G = the limb's phase, B = ambient
  occlusion (a Cycles bake of the whole tree), A = a species mask. On a birch the mask is
  *dark bark*: 1 on thin limbs and twigs, on the roots, on the rough black base of the
  trunk (the lowest metre or so of the old tree, in ragged tongues) and in a chevron under
  each limb that leaves a white stem; 0 on the smooth white trunk and leaders. On a spruce
  it is lichen. The white wood comes first in the index buffer (`barkCoreIndices`), so a
  low tier can stop before the fine limbs and hanging shoots.
- **Foliage sites.** A tree GLB stores no leaves. It stores a point cloud of sites —
  position, orientation, size, wind response, a sky-visibility term and a bent normal —
  and the runtime instances the shared sprays onto them.
- **Leaves are geometry, not alpha cards** (`summer_meadow/leaves.py`). A birch leaf is
  triangular-ovate with a drawn-out point and three teeth a side, folded along its midrib;
  the hero's spray hangs them all round a metre of twig, each facing its own way. Vertex
  colour: R = distance from the leaf base (flutter weight), G = per-leaf id, B = shade
  inside the spray, A = 1 on leaves / 0 on wood. The spruce sections are the Golden
  Forest's (`golden_forest/needles.py`).
- **Sprite sheet.** `summer-impostors.png` is a Cycles orthographic render of each grove
  tree with an emission material that stores data instead of colour: R = shade, G = leaf
  mask, B = hue seed (on bark: 0.5 + half the species mask), A = coverage.

## How the props are made

Props are built as arrays, part by part (`summer_meadow/kit.py`, `cottage.py`,
`maypole.py`, `greenery.py`, `props.py`); Blender only bakes their ambient occlusion.
They are glTF Y-up metres with the base at y = 0 and the front toward +Z.

- **One material shades them all**, so every vertex says what it is made of.
  `COLOR_0`: R = tone (a per-part or per-vertex brightness variation; on a flower, its hue
  seed, always the middle of one of seven equal bands), G = material code / 8, B = baked
  ambient occlusion, A = wear (weathered timber, moss on tiles, lichen on stone, soot on
  the chimney; on leaves and petals 0 at the stalk to 1 at the tip). Codes: 0 weathered
  grey timber, 1 Falu-red boards, 2 white paint, 3 roof tiles, 4 stone and brick, 5 window
  glass, 6 door paint, 7 garland leaves, 8 flowers.
- **`TEXCOORD_0`** lays each surface out flat in metres, stored as 0.5 + metres / 32:
  v runs along the grain of a board (up a wall, along a rail, up a roof slope from the
  eave) and u across it. Glass, leaves and flowers are mapped 0..1 across themselves.
- **The cottage** (9.4 × 5.8 m, ridge 6.2 m, roof 40°) is board-and-batten with every
  batten modelled as relief, white corner boards, bargeboards and window casings, a stone
  plinth, clay pantiles whose rolls are geometry (the courses are left to the shader: v
  counts metres up the slope), half-round ridge tiles, a brick chimney and a gabled porch
  with a cross-braced railing. Ten six-pane windows have their frames and glazing bars in
  front of recessed glass. The boathouse is the same carpentry at 2.6 × 3.4 m.
- **The maypole** is a pole and cross-arm clothed in sprigs of birch (each leaf a folded
  kite) over a dark leafy core, with a leafy rope from its top to each end of the arm, a
  flowered wreath hanging under each end and a tuft of birch on its head. It carries no
  ribbons: those are the runtime's.
- **The rowboat** is the Golden Forest's lofted clinker hull with its outside strakes
  painted; **the boulders** follow that pack's recipe on a 720-triangle sphere; **the
  jetty** is built plank by plank; **the fence** is one three-metre section of a
  roundpole fence that tiles end to end.
- **Anchors.** Each prop's node `extras` (and its record in the manifest) carry its size
  and named points in its own metres: the maypole's `top`, `armLeft`, `armRight`,
  `wreathLeft`, `wreathRight`; the cottage's `chimneyTop`, `door`, `windows`; the jetty's
  `end` and `mooring`; and so on.

## Format

GLBs are written by the generator itself (`fall_grove/glb.py`), not by Blender's exporter:
positions are normalised 16-bit integers restored by the node's scale and translation,
normals and colours are 8-bit (`KHR_mesh_quantization`). There are no materials, textures
or animations in the files; the theme owns every material.

## Regenerating

```bash
blender --background --factory-startup --python scripts/blender/summer_meadow_assets.py -- \
    --out src/themes/summer/assets [--only trees,foliage,impostors,props] [--preview <dir>]
```

`--factory-startup` keeps the run independent of any Blender session that is open.
Growth and props are seeded and the bakes run on the CPU: on the machine that wrote this
pack, three runs in a row produced all twelve files (and the manifest) byte for byte.
Re-run the tests after regenerating. The whole pack takes about fifteen seconds on a
laptop CPU; `--preview` adds a few minutes of stills.
