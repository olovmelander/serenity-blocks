# Winter theme — asset attribution

## arctic-fox.glb — project original (no third-party attribution required)

The white arctic fox that goes its round over the snowfield, and whose shape the fox of light
in the sky is drawn from. It is modelled, not scanned or generated by a model:
`scripts/blender/winter_arctic_fox.py` builds the animal in headless Blender as a signed distance
field of ellipsoids and round cones (the meshing is the Grainfields horse's,
`scripts/blender/grainfields/horse_sdf.py`, and the coat's tools the polar bear's) round the joints
of the theme's own rig (`src/themes/winter/winter-fox-rig.js`), meshes it with surface nets,
decimates and mirrors it. Then `scripts/winter/rig-fox.mjs` gives that mesh the skeleton the theme
animates, the 21 axis-aligned bones of the rig, with skin weights laid by region, and paints the
coat into the vertex colours: how far along the tail a vertex is, how long its fur is, how dark it
is (nose, eyes, the hollows of the ears) and how much of the sky it sees (an occlusion bake).

The file holds no animation: the theme solves every pose itself (`winter-fox-rig.js`, from the pose
`winter-fox-mind.js` resolves) and `src/themes/winter/winter-fox.js` copies the rotations onto the
bones; it is shaded by the theme's own unlit node materials (skin and shells of fur). Regenerate
with

    node scripts/winter/rig-fox.mjs --model [--preview=<dir>]

(Blender 5.2; the same Blender and scripts give the same bytes). `node scripts/winter/rig-fox.mjs`
on the asset itself only redoes skin and coat; `--check` compares a fresh rig with the file (a test
runs it). License: proprietary project original.

## polar-bear.glb — project original (no third-party attribution required)

The polar bear that walks the frozen lake. It is modelled, not scanned or generated by a
model: `scripts/blender/winter_polar_bear.py` builds the animal in headless Blender as a
signed distance field of ellipsoids and round cones (the meshing is the Grainfields horse's,
`scripts/blender/grainfields/horse_sdf.py`) round the joints of the theme's own rig
(`src/themes/winter/winter-bear-rig.js`), meshes it with surface nets, decimates and mirrors it,
and bakes per vertex the part of the body it belongs to and its paint (the black pads, nose,
eyes, lips and claws; how long the hair is; ambient occlusion traced through the field). Then
`scripts/winter/model-bear.mjs` skins that mesh to the rig's 29 axis-aligned bones and writes the
file. It holds no animation: the theme solves every pose itself. Regenerate with

    node scripts/winter/model-bear.mjs [--preview=<dir>]

(Blender 5.2; the same Blender and scripts give the same bytes). License: proprietary project
original.

## snow-ghosts.bin, snow-ghosts-manifest.json — generated

The snow-loaded spruces are generated, not modelled or scanned: `scripts/winter/bake-ghosts.mjs`
grows each of five kinds as a smooth union of ellipsoids (a rimed stem, whorls of drooping
boughs under their pillows, a top bowed over by its load), meshes the field with naive surface
nets at four levels of detail, and stores for every vertex its position (16-bit), its normal
(8-bit) and four shading values read off the field: openness to the sky, bough or snow,
thinness, height. No photograph, scan or third-party model is involved, so there is nothing to
attribute. Regenerate with

    node scripts/winter/bake-ghosts.mjs

which rewrites both files (the same script gives the same bytes; `--check` compares a fresh bake
with the manifest, `--dry --preview=<dir>` ray-casts every kind to one PNG on the CPU).
`tests/unit/winter-ghosts.test.js` pins the manifest's hash to the asset.

_The earlier theme's conifers (`spruce.glb`, `pine.glb`, `fir.glb` and their `_lod` copies) were
removed with it._
