# Neon District source art

The shopfront paintings the Neon District theme shows (shown to players as **Neon Quarter** since
2026-10-26; the id, folders and files keep `neon-district`). They are not shipped as they are:
`scripts/neon-district/build-atlases.mjs` packs them into a WebP atlas under
`public/textures/neon-district/`, paints the campaign (billboard) atlas beside it, and writes the
cell table `src/themes/neon-district/neon-district-atlas.js`.

```sh
node scripts/neon-district/build-atlases.mjs
# or, to try a change without touching the shipped files:
node scripts/neon-district/build-atlases.mjs --out=<dir>
# or, to look at some campaign cells alone (one PNG each, by id prefix):
node scripts/neon-district/build-atlases.mjs --out=<dir> --cells=street-,brand-cascade
```

The bake is deterministic: the same sources give byte-identical atlases. It draws in a headless
page of the installed Chrome with the GPU off (canvas 2D only; set `CHROME_PATH` if Chrome is not
in its usual folder).

| Files | In the atlas |
| --- | --- |
| `storefront_03.jpg` to `storefront_18.jpg` | All sixteen, in `shopfronts.webp` |

`storefront_02` (its sign showed another game's characters) was removed on 2026-11-03; the
numbering keeps its gap.

The shopfronts were generated by the project owner with Google Gemini, on the owner's personal
Google account: `storefront_02` to `07` on 2025-12-11 and `storefront_08` to `18` the same day
(they used to sit in `public/textures/synthcity/` beside the SynthCity texture set, which the
rebuilt theme no longer uses). Record a new piece's tool and date here when you add one. To add a
piece, drop it here, add it to the cell list at the top of the bake script and run the bake.

The billboard adverts that used to sit here (`ads_large_06` to `18`, also Gemini pictures) are
gone: since 2026-11-03 every billboard is painted by the bake.

## The painted campaigns

`billboards.webp` (4096 × 3584, with a 2048 × 1792 half) is painted by the bake on a 2D canvas,
from the campaign list in `src/themes/neon-district/neon-district-campaigns.js` and the street
adverts in the bake script (`STREET_ADS`):

- **Street adverts**: ten one-off campaigns with no shop of their own, four 16:9 screens and six
  squares, named in the district brands' way after the game's own words: Hold Neural (implants:
  a head in profile and the thought it keeps in a hold box), Well FM (night radio: looking down a
  lit well), Rotate Bionics (an arm drawn as a blueprint), Shift Faceplates (a faceted mask a
  step to the side of itself), the game's own poster (its symbol as a neon sign over the
  wordmark, both from `public/assets/branding/`), Paws & Parts (a robot pet clinic), the night
  market, Glance (pay with a look: an iris in a scanner), Lumen Soda and Overclock Repairs.

- **District brands**: one 16:9 billboard and two 2:3 posters per brand, each with the brand's
  mark, its two colours, its name and slogan. The second poster (`brand-<id>-portrait-2`) is the
  same campaign in another design: a product close-up (Cascade), a comic strip (Combo), type
  alone (Stack), a parcel label (Drop), a price tag (Level), the roof sign (Next), an eye chart
  (Clear), a wall of washer doors (Spin).
- **Sukha**: a billboard and a poster that leave an empty, dark ring area (`ring` in the cell
  table) for the theme's shader to breathe in.
- **Serenity Transit**: 34 world posters (2:3) and three mode posters (16:9). Each world poster
  is built round the game's own render of that world (captures under `docs/`), and takes its
  colours from it (a dominant and an accent hue sampled from the picture). They come in seven
  designs, as if by seven designers on one line — `vintage` (a framed print), `side` (full bleed,
  the name up the side), `swiss` (a colour field, an offset square picture, a big line number),
  `deco` (an arched window over sunburst rays), `duotone` (the picture posterised onto two inks,
  the name knocked out of a block), `ticket` (a boarding pass with a split-flap destination) and
  `porthole` (round glass in a brass ring, the line's name set round it) — used about equally and
  never twice in a row (the bake checks this). Every one carries the line's roundel. The three
  mode posters (from the menu's key art in `public/assets/menu-art/`, cropped clear of the board
  and its panels) each have their own design too. The pictures, regions, focus points, names,
  straplines and designs are listed in the bake script (`TRANSIT_WORLDS`, `TRANSIT_MODES`).

Type is the game's own (`public/fonts/`, all OFL): Unbounded, Orbitron, Manrope and Space Mono.
No real company, product, film or game is named or drawn, and no mark is built from four
squares or any other piece shape. Nothing in the campaign atlas comes from an image generator.
